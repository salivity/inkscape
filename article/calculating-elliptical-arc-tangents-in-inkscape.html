Calculating Elliptical Arc Tangents in Inkscape

This article explains the mathematical and algorithmic process Inkscape uses to calculate the tangent vector at any point along an elliptical arc. Inkscape delegates this task to its internal 2D computational geometry engine, lib2geom, which converts SVG arc descriptions into a center-based parametric representation, computes the first derivative of the curve equation with respect to the arc parameter, applies affine transformations, and normalizes the resulting vector.

1. Converting Endpoint to Center Parameterization

The SVG specification defines elliptical arcs using endpoint parameterization: the starting point \((x_1, y_1)\), endpoint \((x_2, y_2)\), radii \((r_x, r_y)\), x-axis rotation angle \(\phi\), large-arc flag \(f_A\), and sweep flag \(f_S\).

To calculate derivatives efficiently, Inkscape converts these values into center parameterization:

  • Ellipse center \((c_x, c_y)\)
  • Radii \(r_x\) and \(r_y\) (corrected for scale if the endpoints cannot be bridged by the specified radii)
  • Rotation angle \(\phi\)
  • Initial angle \(\theta_1\) and angular span \(\Delta\theta\)

Through this conversion, any point along the arc is mapped to a parameter \(t \in [0, 1]\), where the current angular position is \(\theta(t) = \theta_1 + t \cdot \Delta\theta\).

2. Parametric Formulation of the Arc

A point \(P(t)\) on the unrotated, centered ellipse is given by:

\[p(t) = \begin{pmatrix} r_x \cos(\theta(t)) \\ r_y \sin(\theta(t)) \end{pmatrix}\]

Accounting for the ellipse's rotation \(\phi\) and translation to \((c_x, c_y)\), the full curve equation is:

\[P(t) = \begin{pmatrix} c_x \\ c_y \end{pmatrix} + R(\phi) \cdot p(t)\]

where \(R(\phi)\) is the 2D rotation matrix:

\[R(\phi) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\ \sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{pmatrix}\]

3. Evaluating the First Derivative

The tangent vector corresponds to the instantaneous direction of motion along the curve, which is the first derivative of \(P(t)\) with respect to \(t\):

\[\mathbf{T}_{\text{raw}}(t) = \frac{dP}{dt} = R(\phi) \cdot \frac{d}{dt} \begin{pmatrix} r_x \cos(\theta(t)) \\ r_y \sin(\theta(t)) \end{pmatrix}\]

Applying the chain rule with \(\frac{d\theta}{dt} = \Delta\theta\):

\[\frac{d}{dt} p(t) = \Delta\theta \begin{pmatrix} -r_x \sin(\theta(t)) \\ r_y \cos(\theta(t)) \end{pmatrix}\]

Multiplying by the rotation matrix yields the unnormalized tangent vector:

\[\mathbf{T}_{\text{raw}}(t) = \Delta\theta \begin{pmatrix} -r_x \cos\phi \sin(\theta(t)) - r_y \sin\phi \cos(\theta(t)) \\ -r_x \sin\phi \sin(\theta(t)) + r_y \cos\phi \cos(\theta(t)) \end{pmatrix}\]

The sign of \(\Delta\theta\) directly preserves the traversal direction established by the SVG sweep-flag.

4. Normalization and Transformation Handling

To obtain a directional unit tangent vector \(\hat{\mathbf{T}}(t)\), Inkscape divides the raw derivative vector by its Euclidean norm:

\[\hat{\mathbf{T}}(t) = \frac{\mathbf{T}_{\text{raw}}(t)}{\|\mathbf{T}_{\text{raw}}(t)\|}\]

If the arc belongs to an object with inherited SVG transform matrices (such as non-uniform scaling or skewing), Inkscape transforms the vector by the linear portion of the cumulative transformation matrix and normalizes it again to produce the final screen-space or world-space tangent.
