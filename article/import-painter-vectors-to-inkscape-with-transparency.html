Import Painter Vectors to Inkscape with Transparency

Moving vector artwork from Corel Painter to Inkscape while preserving alpha channels and opacity settings can be challenging because standard EPS exports inherently flatten translucent data. This guide explains how to bypass flattening limitations by isolating your shape layers, utilizing modern container formats, and importing your assets cleanly into Inkscape with active transparency intact.

1. Separate Vectors from Painter Dynamic Layers

Corel Painter treats vector objects as dedicated "Shape" layers. To avoid raster merging:

  • Open the Layers panel in Painter.
  • Ensure all vector shapes remain unconverted (do not select "Commit" or "Convert to Default Layer").
  • Group your vector shapes separately from dynamic raster effects, watercolors, or impasto layers, as these raster elements force Painter to flatten overlaps during standard vector export.

2. Export via PDF to Maintain Alpha Transparency

The standard "Save As > EPS" route in Painter relies on PostScript Level 2 or 3, which cannot process native vector alpha channels and forces flattening into opaque bounding boxes. Instead, use a PDF-based export workflow:

  • Go to File > Print.
  • Select a modern virtual printer such as Microsoft Print to PDF or Adobe PDF.
  • In the print properties, set the output to maintain document dimensions and ensure color management does not compress transparency layers into a single raster background.
  • Print the document to generate a high-compatibility PDF file. PDF specifications (version 1.4 and above) natively support transparent vector fills and strokes without rasterizing paths.

3. Alternative Method: Exporting via Layered PSD

If your project contains a mix of vector shapes and transparent brushwork that you want to manipulate as separate elements:

  • Go to File > Save As and choose Photoshop (*.psd).
  • In the PSD export dialog, confirm that layers are preserved.
  • Painter writes vector paths and transparent layer boundaries into the PSD structure. Inkscape natively reads PSD files, parsing individual layers and maintaining non-destructive opacity.

4. Importing into Inkscape

Once the file is generated:

  1. Open Inkscape and select File > Import.
  2. Select your exported PDF or PSD.
  3. If importing a PDF, choose the Poppler/Cairo import or the Internal import engine. Check the option to Import clip paths and ensure Embed images is selected only if you are also bringing along raster textures.
  4. Click OK.

5. Inspect and Restore Alpha Channels in Inkscape

After importing, your artwork will arrive bundled in a group:

  • Select the artwork and press Ctrl + Shift + G (or right-click and choose Ungroup) to access individual paths.
  • Open the Fill and Stroke panel (Ctrl + Shift + F).
  • Click on individual shapes to confirm their Opacity slider or the RGBA alpha channel value. You can now adjust or reapply transparency directly in Inkscape without any background clipping artifacts.
