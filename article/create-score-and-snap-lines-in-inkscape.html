Create Score and Snap Lines in Inkscape

This guide explains how to design precise score-and-snap lines for acrylic sheet fabrication using Inkscape. Scoring allows you to create shallow, controlled surface cuts—either using a low-power laser pass or as vector guides for manual scoring tools—enabling clean, straight breaks along designated axes. By correctly setting up your vector strokes, color codes, and alignment tools, you can prepare production-ready vector files for your acrylic projects.

Step 1: Set Up Document Dimensions and Units

  1. Open Inkscape and navigate to File > Document Properties (or press Ctrl+Shift+D).
  2. Under the Display tab, set Display units to your preferred unit of measure (inches or millimeters).
  3. Under Custom size, set the page dimensions to match your acrylic sheet or the working bed of your laser cutter.

Step 2: Draw the Score Lines

  1. Select the Draw Bezier curves and straight lines tool (press B or Shift+F6).
  2. Click once on the canvas where the cut should begin, hold Ctrl to constrain the angle to a perfectly straight horizontal or vertical line, and double-click to finish the line.
  3. If you are creating a manual scoring template, extend the lines slightly past the boundary of the workpiece so your alignment marks remain visible once the scoring blade is in position.

Step 3: Configure Stroke Style and Color Coding

Laser software (such as LightBurn, RDWorks, or Glowforge) differentiates between through-cuts, scores, and engravings using distinct vector colors and line weights.

  1. Select your line and open the Fill and Stroke panel (Ctrl+Shift+F).
  2. Go to the Stroke paint tab:
    • Set your score lines to a distinct color, such as solid blue (#0000FF), while keeping through-cut boundaries colored differently, such as solid red (#FF0000).
  3. Go to the Stroke style tab:
    • Set the Width to a hairline value suitable for vector cutting, typically 0.001 in (0.025 mm) or 0.1 mm depending on your laser controller's requirements.
    • If you are cutting by hand with an acrylic knife and printing a paper template, increase the stroke width to 0.5 pt or 1 pt for easy visibility.

Step 4: Position and Align with Snapping

  1. Enable Snapping from the top-right toolbar or press %.
  2. Enable snapping to paths, edges, and bounding boxes.
  3. Open the Align and Distribute panel (Ctrl+Shift+A).
  4. Select the score line alongside your main part outline, then use the alignment tools to center or offset the line to the exact cut dimension required.

Step 5: Export the File for Fabrication

  1. Check that all objects are paths by selecting all elements (Ctrl+A) and clicking Path > Object to Path (Shift+Ctrl+C).
  2. Save your master file as an Inkscape SVG.
  3. Export the file to your machine’s required format:
    • For most laser cutters, save a copy as Plain SVG or DXF (File > Save As).
    • For physical paper printouts, save as a PDF to ensure exact 1:1 scale reproduction.
