How to Draw Internal Tangents to Circles in Inkscape

Finding the common internal tangent lines between two non-overlapping circles in Inkscape can be accomplished either through automated snapping tools or via classic geometric construction. While modern versions of Inkscape include tangent snapping features for quick alignment, geometric construction guarantees mathematical precision when rendering vector graphics. This guide outlines both the fast snapping technique and the exact geometric workflow.

Method 1: Using Inkscape's Tangent Snapping

This is the fastest method and relies on Inkscape’s built-in geometric snapping engine.

  1. Enable Snapping: Click the Enable Snapping toggle in the top-right corner of the interface (or press %).
  2. Enable Tangent Snapping: Expand the Snap Controls popover. Under the Points and Paths sections, ensure Snap to paths and Snap to tangents are turned on.
  3. Select the Pen Tool: Press B or select the Draw Bezier curves and straight lines tool from the toolbar.
  4. Draw the First Internal Tangent:
    • Hover over the inner side of the first circle until the snap tooltip indicates a tangent or path intersection.
    • Click once to start the line.
    • Cross over to the opposite side of the second circle. Move the cursor along the perimeter until the snap indicator displays Tangent to path.
    • Click to set the endpoint, then press Enter to finalize the line.
  5. Draw the Second Internal Tangent: Repeat the process starting from the opposite diagonal side to create the crossing tangent line.

Method 2: Mathematical Construction (Exact Precision)

If snapping produces floating-point rounding errors or fails to lock onto both curves simultaneously, use the Euclidean construction method.

Step 1: Connect Circle Centers

  1. Ensure both circles are paths (Path > Object to Path or Shift + Ctrl + C).
  2. Turn on Snap to object centers.
  3. With the Pen Tool (B), draw a straight segment connecting the center of Circle A (\(C_1\), radius \(R_1\)) to the center of Circle B (\(C_2\), radius \(R_2\)).

Step 2: Construct the Sum Circle

  1. Select Circle A (the larger circle).
  2. Duplicate it (Ctrl + D).
  3. Open the Transform panel (Shift + Ctrl + M) to the Scale tab.
  4. Increase the diameter of the duplicate circle so that its radius equals \(R_1 + R_2\).
  5. Re-center this new enlarged circle over Circle A’s center.

Step 3: Find the Midpoint Between Centers

  1. Select the segment connecting \(C_1\) and \(C_2\).
  2. Duplicate it (Ctrl + D) and rotate it 90 degrees to visually locate the exact midpoint, or use the Snap to midpoints of line segments option.
  3. Draw a reference circle centered at this midpoint with a diameter equal to the distance between \(C_1\) and \(C_2\).

Step 4: Locate Intersection Points

  1. Identify where the reference circle (from Step 3) intersects the enlarged circle (from Step 2). There will be two intersection points (\(P_1\) and \(P_2\)).
  2. Draw a line from \(C_1\) through intersection point \(P_1\) to the edge of the original Circle A. The point where this line crosses the boundary of Circle A is the exact point of tangency on Circle A.

Step 5: Draw the Tangent

  1. Select the Pen Tool (B).
  2. Draw a perpendicular line from the point of tangency on Circle A across to Circle B.
  3. With Snap to perpendicular lines enabled, lock onto the boundary of Circle B to complete the exact internal tangent line.
  4. Repeat for \(P_2\) to generate the opposite internal tangent.
  5. Delete the temporary construction circles and reference lines.
