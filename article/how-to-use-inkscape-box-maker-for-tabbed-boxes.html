How to Use Inkscape Box Maker for Tabbed Boxes

Creating finger-jointed tabbed enclosure boxes in Inkscape is a straightforward process using the Box Maker extension, commonly used for laser cutting templates. This guide explains how to access the extension, input critical parameters such as internal dimensions, material thickness, and kerf, generate the flat-pack cut files, and prepare the vector layout for physical production.

Step 1: Open the Box Maker Extension

  1. Launch Inkscape with a new document.
  2. Navigate to the top menu and select Extensions > Render > Box Maker (in some Inkscape distributions or extension packs, this may be listed under Extensions > Laser Tools > Tabbed Box).

Step 2: Configure Dimensions

In the extension pop-up window, configure the size of your enclosure:

  • Dimensions Unit: Set your preferred unit of measurement (millimeters or inches).
  • Length, Width, and Height: Enter the desired dimensions for the finished box.
  • Inside vs. Outside Dimensions: Check whether the values represent the internal volume or external boundary. Choose "Inside" if components must fit inside with precise clearance, or "Outside" if the exterior dimensions must not exceed a specific footprint.

Step 3: Input Material Thickness and Kerf

Accurate material measurements are essential for tight finger joints:

  • Material Thickness: Measure your sheet material (acrylic, plywood, or MDF) using digital calipers and enter the exact measurement. Nominal thicknesses often vary from actual measurements.
  • Kerf: Enter the kerf value, which is the width of material removed by the laser beam (typically between 0.1 mm and 0.2 mm depending on the laser cutter and material). Adding kerf ensures the tabs create a tight, friction-fit joint without requiring excessive glue.

Step 4: Adjust Tab Settings

  • Tab/Finger Width: Define the width of the individual interlocking tabs. A common rule of thumb is to make tabs 2 to 3 times the material thickness for structural integrity.
  • Tab Symmetry / Layout: Leave settings on symmetrical tabs unless your design specifically requires an alternating layout.

Step 5: Generate and Arrange the Panels

  1. Click Apply and then Close the Box Maker dialog.
  2. The extension will generate six distinct flat panels on the canvas: top, bottom, and four sides.
  3. Select the generated parts and ungroup them (Object > Ungroup or Ctrl+Shift+G) if you need to reposition panels to fit your stock sheet or add cutouts for ports, buttons, or ventilation.

Step 6: Prepare the File for Laser Cutting

  1. Select all paths and open the Fill and Stroke panel (Ctrl+Shift+F).
  2. Remove any fill by setting Fill to No paint.
  3. Set the Stroke paint to a solid color required by your laser software for vector cuts (typically pure red: #FF0000 or black: #000000).
  4. Set the Stroke width to a hairline thickness, commonly 0.001 in or 0.05 mm.
  5. Save your file in the required format for your laser software, such as standard SVG or DXF.
