How to Use the Symbols Library in Inkscape

Inkscape includes a built-in Symbols Library that allows you to quickly browse, select, and drag and drop pre-made vector icons and graphics directly onto your canvas. This guide explains how to access the Symbols dialog, choose from various pre-installed icon sets, add symbols to your document, and modify them for your design projects.

Step 1: Open the Symbols Dialog

To access the library of pre-made icons:

  1. Open your document in Inkscape.
  2. Go to the top menu and select Object > Symbols.
  3. Alternatively, press the keyboard shortcut Shift + Ctrl + Y (Windows/Linux) or Shift + Cmd + Y (macOS).

The Symbols dockable panel will appear on the right side of your workspace.

Step 2: Select a Symbol Category

Inkscape comes preloaded with several standard symbol sets, including logic symbols, flowchart elements, and AIGA icons:

  1. Locate the dropdown menu at the top of the Symbols panel (it defaults to "Current document").
  2. Click the dropdown to view available icon sets, such as AIGA Symbol Signs, Flowchart, or Logic Symbols.
  3. Select a category to display all available vector shapes in the preview area below.

Step 3: Drag and Drop Icons to the Canvas

Once you find the icon you want to use:

  1. Click and hold the desired symbol from the preview panel.
  2. Drag your cursor over to the canvas area.
  3. Release the mouse button to place the icon into your project.

Step 4: Resize and Reposition

After placing the symbol:

  1. Switch to the Select and transform objects tool (shortcut: S).
  2. Click the symbol on the canvas to display its bounding box handles.
  3. Drag the corner handles to resize it. Hold Ctrl while dragging to maintain the icon's original aspect ratio.

Step 5: Edit and Customize the Icon

By default, inserted symbols act as cloned objects:

  1. Select the placed icon on your canvas.
  2. Go to Edit > Clone > Unlink Clone (or press Alt + Shift + D).
  3. Once unlinked, you can change the fill color, stroke properties, or edit individual nodes using standard Inkscape tools.
