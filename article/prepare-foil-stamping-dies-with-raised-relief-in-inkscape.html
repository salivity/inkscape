Prepare Foil Stamping Dies with Raised Relief in Inkscape

This guide outlines how to prepare production-ready vector artwork in Inkscape for combination hot-foil stamping and raised-relief (embossing) dies. You will learn how to configure document settings, convert dynamic elements into clean paths, establish layer separations for foil and relief depths, and adhere to essential mechanical tolerances required for precision CNC milling and chemical etching.

1. Set Proper Document Dimensions and Units

Create a new document sized to your exact final trim or die-plate dimensions. Open File > Document Properties and set both the Display units and Page size units to millimeters (mm) or inches (in). Ensure the scale is set strictly to 1.0 to guarantee the physical dimensions sent to the die-maker match your design precisely.

2. Convert All Fonts and Strokes to Outlines

Die-making machinery cannot process live fonts, text objects, or open stroke outlines; it requires closed vector boundaries.

  • Convert Text: Select any text objects and navigate to Path > Object to Path (Shift+Ctrl+C).
  • Convert Strokes: Select any lines or objects utilizing stroke outlines and choose Path > Stroke to Path (Ctrl+Alt+C).
  • Verify Closed Shapes: Switch to the Node Tool (N) and inspect the paths to confirm that every element consists of a closed vector contour with solid fills and no active strokes.

3. Clean and Merge Vector Paths

Die-cutting and engraving software can fail if paths overlap or contain self-intersecting loops.

  • Select overlapping shapes meant to be stamped together and run Path > Union (Ctrl++).
  • Eliminate tiny stray nodes and unwanted points by going to Path > Simplify sparingly, or manually clean them using the Node Tool.
  • Check your line integrity by going to View > Display mode > Outline. This wireframe view reveals hidden lines, stray vectors, and interior crossings that must be deleted or merged.

4. Separate Foil and Relief Artwork into Dedicated Layers

Combination dies involve two processes: applying foil and raising the paper fibers (blind or registered embossing). These actions require distinct depth profiles on the physical brass or magnesium die. Open Layer > Layers and Objects (Shift+Ctrl+L) and structure your design into dedicated, aligned layers:

  • Layer 1: Foil Area: Place the artwork that will receive the hot-stamping foil here. Use solid 100% black (#000000) fills for this geometry.
  • Layer 2: Raised Relief (Embossing): Place the areas that will physically push the substrate upward here. For single-level flat-top raised areas, use solid 100% black fills. For multi-level or sculptured dies requiring varying depths, specify distinct tonal values (e.g., 20%, 40%, 60% black) or assign distinct CMYK spot colors mapped to specific depths per your die-maker’s technical specifications.
  • Layer 3: Die Line / Registration: Include a 1-point stroke outlining the exact trim/cut edge of the sheet to ensure the engraver correctly aligns the die relative to the printed sheet.

5. Adhere to Minimum Tolerances for Tooling

Hot-foil dies require sufficient surface area for heat retention and release, while relief dies need adequate clearance to prevent tearing the paper stock.

  • Minimum Line Weight (Foil): Keep all standalone foil lines at a minimum width of 0.2 mm (~0.5 pt). Fine serif details narrower than this will cause flaking or fail to transfer cleanly.
  • Minimum Line Spacing (Foil Trapping): Ensure negative space (reverses) between foil elements is at least 0.3 mm (~0.8 pt) to prevent the foil from filling in during heat activation.
  • Relief Bevel Allowance: Raised elements expand slightly on the back of the die to form a bevel. Maintain at least 0.4 mm to 0.5 mm separation between the edge of a raised relief area and any adjacent flat graphics to prevent shearing the paper.

6. Export the Final Vector File

Ensure no clipping paths, bitmap images, drop shadows, or Gaussian blur filters exist in your file.

  1. Navigate to File > Save As...
  2. Choose Portable Document Format (*.pdf).
  3. In the PDF export dialogue, set the PDF version to PDF 1.4 or PDF 1.5 to avoid unsupported transparency formats.
  4. Ensure the option Convert texts to paths is checked as a safeguard.
  5. Provide the vendor with the exported PDF along with a mechanical key detailing the layer designations, target substrate caliper, and the intended relief depth.
