Laser Focal Height Compensation in Inkscape

Calculating laser focal height compensation for curved surfaces ensures that the laser beam maintains its optimal spot size and power density across varying material elevations. Because Inkscape is inherently a two-dimensional vector design tool, compensating for a curved surface requires mapping the three-dimensional elevation profile of the workpiece to specific X-Y coordinates, calculating the necessary vertical adjustments, and segmenting the vector paths into distinct focal zones or exporting parametric Z-axis G-code.

1. Determine Lens Depth of Focus and Surface Profile

Before designing in Inkscape, calculate your laser lens's usable Depth of Focus (DoF) to determine how much vertical deviation is acceptable before the beam loses cutting efficiency:

\[\text{DoF} \approx 2 \times \left( \frac{\lambda \cdot f^2}{\pi \cdot w_0^2} \right)\]

For most standard 2.0-inch (50.8 mm) CO2 laser lenses, the practical depth of focus is approximately \(\pm 1.0\text{ mm}\) to \(\pm 1.5\text{ mm}\).

Next, measure the vertical drop (\(\Delta Z\)) of your curved workpiece along its X or Y axis. For a spherical or cylindrical surface of radius \(R\):

\[\Delta Z(x) = R - \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}\]

Where \(x\) is the horizontal distance from the apex of the curve.

2. Segment the Design into Focal Zones in Inkscape

When using a standard laser controller that supports layer-based Z-offsets (such as Ruida with motorized Z-beds or machines driven by LightBurn), segment the 2D cut vectors in Inkscape into step-wise focus layers:

  1. Calculate Zone Widths: Divide the total height change into increments equal to your lens’s safe focus tolerance (e.g., \(1.0\text{ mm}\) intervals). Using your surface curve equation, solve for the \(X\) coordinates where each vertical step occurs.
  2. Import or Draw the Cutting Geometry: Place the primary flat pattern on the canvas.
  3. Slice the Vectors:
    • Draw vertical or horizontal guide lines at the calculated \(X\) coordinate boundaries.
    • Use Path > Cut Path (Ctrl+Alt+K) to split the cutting vectors where they intersect the boundary lines.
  4. Assign Colors to Elevation Layers: Move each segmented set of vectors to a distinct layer or assign unique stroke colors (e.g., Red for \(Z = 0\text{ mm}\), Blue for \(Z = -1.0\text{ mm}\), Green for \(Z = -2.0\text{ mm}\)).
  5. Set Offsets in Controller Software: Map each color to a specific Z-offset or manual bed focus height during post-processing.

3. Continuous 3D Path Compensation Using Inkscape Gcodetools

For CNC-based lasers with active 3-axis motion (X, Y, and dynamic Z), generate continuously compensated toolpaths using Inkscape's Gcodetools extension:

  1. Prepare the Path: Convert all objects to paths using Path > Object to Path (Shift+Ctrl+C).
  2. Configure Orientation: Go to Extensions > Gcodetools > Orientation points. Set the mode to "2-points mode" and define your working coordinates.
  3. Map the Surface Profile:
    • Open Extensions > Gcodetools > Tools library and select Laser or Cylinder.
    • Open Extensions > Gcodetools > Surface. Set the mathematical function representing your surface curve (e.g., matching the curve radius formula relative to the origin).
  4. Generate Dynamic Z G-code:
    • Open Extensions > Gcodetools > Path to Gcode.
    • Under the Depth function tab, reference the defined surface profile so that Z coordinates dynamically update along the cut path.
    • Export the .ngc or .gcode file.

4. Speed and Power Scaling for Defocused Tolerances

If cutting a continuous line without a motorized dynamic Z-axis, compensate for the larger beam waist (\(w\)) at the curve's lower regions using Inkscape to scale the line characteristics:

  • Calculate the spot enlargement: \(w(z) = w_0 \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\lambda z}{\pi w_0^2}\right)^2}\).
  • As the spot widens, power density drops proportionally to the area increase. In Inkscape, separate the peripheral path segments and assign lower cutting speeds or higher repeat counts to allow through-cuts despite the slight defocus.
