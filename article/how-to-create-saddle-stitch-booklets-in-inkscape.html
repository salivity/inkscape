How to Create Saddle-Stitch Booklets in Inkscape

Creating a saddle-stitch booklet imposition layout in Inkscape requires organizing your pages into printer spreads rather than sequential reader spreads so that the folded sheets read correctly after binding. This guide outlines the mathematical rules of saddle-stitching, setting up a multi-page document using Inkscape's native Page Tool, organizing content across spreads, and exporting the final artwork for double-sided printing.

1. Understand Saddle-Stitch Imposition Math

Saddle-stitch binding folds printed sheets in half and staples them along the crease. Because each folded sheet of paper contains four distinct booklet pages (two on the front, two on the back), your document must adhere to specific rules:

  • The Multiple of Four Rule: Your total booklet page count must always be a multiple of 4 (e.g., 4, 8, 12, 16, 20). If your content only takes up 14 pages, you must add 2 blank pages (often placed inside the covers or at the very back) to reach 16.
  • Sheet Count: Divide your total page count (\(N\)) by 4 to determine how many physical sheets of paper you need. For a 16-page booklet, you need 4 physical sheets (8 spreads).
  • Page Pairing Formula: On any given spread, the sum of the two facing page numbers must equal the total number of pages plus one (\(N + 1\)). For an 8-page booklet (\(8 + 1 = 9\)):
    • Sheet 1 Front: Page 8 (left) + Page 1 (right)
    • Sheet 1 Back: Page 2 (left) + Page 7 (right)
    • Sheet 2 Front: Page 6 (left) + Page 3 (right)
    • Sheet 2 Back: Page 4 (left) + Page 5 (right)

2. Configure the Document in Inkscape

Inkscape (version 1.2 and later) includes a native Create and Edit Document Pages tool (Shift + P), allowing you to manage multi-page documents directly.

  1. Open a New Document: Go to File > Document Properties (Ctrl + Shift + D).
  2. Set the Spread Dimensions: Set the page orientation to match a two-page flat spread. For example, if your final folded booklet pages are A5 portrait, set your Inkscape page size to A4 landscape. If your pages are US Half-Letter portrait (5.5" x 8.5"), set your spread size to US Letter landscape (11" x 8.5").
  3. Add Spreads: Select the Page Tool from the left toolbar (Shift + P). Click Create a new page in the top control bar until you have enough spreads for your imposition (Total Booklet Pages divided by 2).
  4. Arrange the Pages: In the Page Tool control bar, click Rearrange Pages to align your spreads in a clean grid or single column for easy navigation.

3. Establish Folds, Bleeds, and Margins

  1. Add Center Folds: Drag a vertical guide from the left ruler to the exact center of your spread (e.g., at 5.5 inches on an 11-inch wide page). Lock guides under View > Guides > Lock Guides to avoid shifting them.
  2. Account for Creep: In saddle-stitched booklets, the thickness of the paper forces the inner pages to extend slightly further out than the outer pages when folded. Keep critical text and elements at least 0.25 inches (6 mm) away from outer trim edges to prevent them from being cut off during final booklet trimming.
  3. Apply Bleed: If elements run off the edge of the page, extend them 0.125 inches (3 mm) beyond the page boundaries.

4. Lay Out Content into Printer Spreads

Label each page visually on a separate, temporary layout guide layer to avoid placement errors:

  1. Create a layer called Imposition Map and add temporary text labels to the left and right sides of each spread indicating the page numbers according to your calculated formula.
  2. Place your artwork, headings, and body text onto permanent design layers according to those numbers.
  3. Ensure the front cover (Page 1) and back cover (Page \(N\)) are placed side-by-side on the first spread (Back Cover on the left, Front Cover on the right).
  4. Hide or delete the Imposition Map layer once all assets are locked into their correct positions.

5. Alternative: Sequential Design with Automated PDF Imposition

If manually sorting artwork into printer spreads is too complex, design your document sequentially in Inkscape:

  1. Use the Page Tool to create individual single pages (e.g., A5 portrait or US Half-Letter portrait) numbered sequentially from 1 to \(N\).
  2. Design the booklet in reading order.
  3. Go to File > Save a Copy... and select Portable Document Format (*.pdf).
  4. Open the exported sequential PDF in an imposition utility (such as Adobe Acrobat's "Booklet" print function, pdfbook2, or free tools like Booklet Creator) to convert the sequential PDF into an imposed saddle-stitch print file automatically.

6. Export and Print

  1. Go to File > Save a Copy... and save your imposed layout as a PDF.
  2. When printing the PDF, select Two-Sided Printing / Duplex.
  3. Set the duplex binding setting to Flip on Short Edge (for landscape-oriented spreads).
  4. Fold the printed sheets down the center crease and staple along the spine using a long-reach stapler to complete the saddle-stitch booklet.
