Shaded Relief Mountains in Inkscape Calligraphy Tool

This guide explains how to draw shaded relief mountains for fantasy maps using the Calligraphy tool in Inkscape. By configuring the tool’s angle and pressure dynamics, you can establish a light source, carve out prominent ridgelines, and build hand-drawn shadow strokes that create three-dimensional topographical depth.

1. Configure the Calligraphy Tool Settings

Select the Calligraphy tool from the left toolbar or press C. Choose a dark brown or charcoal gray rather than pure black for a more natural cartographic aesthetic. In the top tool controls bar, adjust the following parameters:

  • Profile: Dip Pen or Marker
  • Width: 15 to 30 (adjust according to your canvas resolution)
  • Thinning: -10 to 10 (keeps line ends slightly tapered)
  • Angle: 30° to 45° (locks the nib to produce wide strokes on downward diagonal pulls and razor-thin strokes on cross-cutting motions)
  • Fixation: 90 to 100 (maintains a consistent nib angle regardless of stroke direction)
  • Caps: 0.0 (provides crisp, chisel-like ends)

2. Determine the Light Source and Main Ridgelines

Cartographic convention dictates lighting from the upper left (northwest). This means the slopes facing southeast will hold the deepest shadows, while northwest-facing slopes remain bright.

Draw the main spine of the mountain range using the Calligraphy tool or the Pen tool (B). Pull the stroke diagonally downward to create jagged, branching spurs. This central line represents the highest crest of your mountain range and serves as the boundary between light and shadow.

3. Render the Shadow Slopes

With the Calligraphy tool active, fill in the leeward (southeast) side of the ridgeline using short, overlapping strokes:

  1. Place your cursor at the primary ridge and pull downward following the fall line of the slope.
  2. Allow the angled nib to lay down thick swathes of color where slopes are steep.
  3. Break up the strokes to create ravines and gullies. Leave small gaps of negative space within the shadow mass to suggest rocky outcroppings and secondary ridges.
  4. Taper the stroke pressure toward the base of the mountain to blend the elevation smoothly into the surrounding lowlands.

4. Detail the Illuminated Slopes

The northwest slopes must remain mostly blank to convey strong sunlight, but sparse detailing prevents them from looking unfinished:

  1. Reduce the Calligraphy tool width by roughly 50%.
  2. Add minimal, fragmented strokes along secondary crests or sharp drop-offs on the lit side.
  3. Keep these strokes short and spaced far apart, ensuring the white or parchment background dominates the slope.

5. Group and Refine

Select all shading strokes and ridgelines, then press Ctrl + G to group them. Set the group’s blend mode to Multiply in the Layers and Objects panel (Ctrl + Shift + L). This allows underlying paper textures, contour lines, or biome colors to show through your ink work. If the shadows appear too harsh, reduce the group opacity to between 80% and 90% for a softer, aged finish.
