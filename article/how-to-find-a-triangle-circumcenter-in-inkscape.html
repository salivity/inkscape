How to Find a Triangle Circumcenter in Inkscape

This guide explains how to locate the circumcenter of any triangle in Inkscape by constructing perpendicular bisectors on its sides. By leveraging Inkscape’s precision snapping system and rotation tools, you can easily determine the exact midpoints of the triangle's segments, project perpendicular lines, and find their mutual point of intersection to establish the circumcircle's center.

Step 1: Draw Your Triangle

  1. Select the Draw Bezier curves and straight lines tool (shortcut: B).
  2. Click three times on the canvas to define your three vertices, then click the starting node to close the path and form a complete triangle.

Step 2: Enable Necessary Snapping Controls

Precision is required to find midpoints and intersections accurately:

  1. Enable Snapping in the top-right corner of the interface (shortcut: %).
  2. Open the Snapping menu and ensure the following options are active:
    • Snap to paths
    • Snap to midpoints of line segments
    • Snap to path intersections
    • Snap to cusp nodes

Step 3: Create the First Perpendicular Bisector

  1. Select the Draw Bezier curves tool (B).
  2. Click on vertex A, then double-click on vertex B to create a straight line segment covering the first edge of your triangle.
  3. Select this new line segment using the Select and transform objects tool (S).
  4. Open the Transform panel via Object > Transform (shortcut: Shift + Ctrl + M) and select the Rotate tab.
  5. Set the angle to 90.0 degrees and click Apply.
  6. Drag this rotated line toward the original edge until its center snaps directly to the Midpoint of the triangle’s edge. Extend the line if necessary by holding Shift while dragging a handle to preserve its orientation.

Step 4: Create the Second Perpendicular Bisector

  1. Repeat the process for a second side of the triangle (between vertex B and vertex C):
    • Use the Bezier tool to trace a line along this second edge.
    • Rotate the new segment by 90.0 degrees using the Transform panel.
    • Move the rotated segment until it snaps onto the Midpoint of the second edge.
  2. Extend both perpendicular lines until they visibly cross each other.

Step 5: Locate the Circumcenter

The point where the two perpendicular bisector lines cross is the circumcenter.

  1. Select the Circles and ellipses tool (E).
  2. Hover over the crossing lines until the indicator displays Path intersection.
  3. Hold Shift + Ctrl while clicking and dragging from this intersection out to any of the triangle's three vertices.
  4. The resulting circle will perfectly intersect all three vertices, verifying that the intersection point is the true circumcenter.
