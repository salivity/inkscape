Convert Line with Round Caps to Outline in Inkscape

Converting an open line with thick round caps into a seamless closed outline in Inkscape is a straightforward process achieved by converting the stroke into a path and adjusting its fill and stroke properties. This technique transforms a single-line stroke into an editable, closed boundary shape, making it ideal for laser cutting, CNC routing, vinyl plotting, or further vector manipulation.

Step 1: Set Up the Open Line

  1. Select the Draw Freehand Lines tool (P) or the Bezier Curves tool (B) and draw your line.
  2. Open the Fill and Stroke panel by pressing Ctrl + Shift + F (or Cmd + Shift + F on macOS).
  3. In the Stroke paint tab, ensure your stroke has a solid color.
  4. In the Stroke style tab, increase the Width to your desired thickness.
  5. Under the Cap options, click the middle icon for Round cap. The ends of your line will become semi-circular.

Step 2: Convert the Stroke to a Path

  1. Select the line using the Select tool (S).
  2. Navigate to the top menu and select Path > Stroke to Path (or press Ctrl + Alt + C).

This action automatically converts the open stroke—including the rounded end caps—into a single, closed, filled vector shape representing the perimeter of the original stroke.

Step 3: Switch from a Filled Shape to an Outline

By default, "Stroke to Path" fills the new shape with your previous stroke color and leaves the shape's stroke empty. To turn it into an outline:

  1. With the object selected, go to the Fill and Stroke panel.
  2. Under the Fill tab, click the X (No paint) icon to remove the interior color.
  3. Under the Stroke paint tab, click the solid square (Flat color) to apply an outline.
  4. In the Stroke style tab, set the outline width to your required measurement.

Step 4: Verify and Clean Up the Nodes

  1. Switch to the Edit paths by nodes tool (N).
  2. Inspect the path. You will see a continuous loop of nodes enclosing the shape seamlessly without gaps.
  3. If the path contains overlapping segments or stray nodes, select the nodes and use Path > Union (Ctrl + +) or delete redundant nodes to ensure a clean, continuous outline.
