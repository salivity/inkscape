How to Sculpt Nodes with the Inkscape Node Tool

This guide explains how to use the Sculpt mode within Inkscape's Node tool to reshape paths organically. By using this feature, you can manipulate complex vector lines and multiple nodes simultaneously with a soft, proportional falloff, allowing you to edit shapes similarly to sculpting digital clay rather than moving individual points one by one.

Step 1: Prepare Your Path

To sculpt nodes, the object must be a vector path. If you are working with a basic shape (such as a circle, rectangle, or star), select it and go to Path > Object to Path (or press Shift + Ctrl + C).

Step 2: Select the Nodes

  1. Activate the Node Tool by clicking its icon in the left toolbar or pressing N (or F2).
  2. Click on your path to reveal its nodes.
  3. Select the group of nodes you want to sculpt. You can click and drag a selection box around them, hold Shift and click individual nodes, or press Ctrl + A to select all nodes on the path.

Tip: For a smoother, more detailed sculpting effect, select your nodes and press Insert on your keyboard to add more nodes along the segment.

Step 3: Sculpt Using the Alt Key

  1. Hover your cursor over the area of selected nodes you wish to pull or push.
  2. Hold down the Alt key.
  3. Click and drag the left mouse button.

As you drag, the node closest to the cursor moves at the full speed of your mouse movement, while adjacent selected nodes move proportionally less based on a smooth, bell-shaped falloff curve.

Controlling the Sculpt Effect

  • Influence Area: The sculpting action only affects the nodes that are currently selected. Unselected nodes remain completely stationary, acting as anchor points.
  • Movement Sensitivity: The effect responds to your zoom level and mouse velocity. Zooming in gives you finer control over a concentrated area, while zooming out allows for broader, sweeping transformations across large paths.
