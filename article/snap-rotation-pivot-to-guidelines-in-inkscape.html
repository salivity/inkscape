Snap Rotation Pivot to Guidelines in Inkscape

Snapping an object's rotation pivot to a guideline intersection in Inkscape allows you to execute precise radial transformations and rotations around a defined coordinate. This guide explains how to enable the necessary snapping options in Inkscape and position the shape's rotation center directly onto crossing guidelines.

Step 1: Create the Guideline Intersection

  1. Click and drag from the top horizontal ruler onto the canvas to place a horizontal guideline.
  2. Click and drag from the left vertical ruler to place a vertical guideline.
  3. Position them so they cross at the exact point where you want your rotation center located.

Step 2: Configure Snapping Settings

Inkscape requires specific snapping toggles to be active for the rotation handle to detect guidelines:

  1. Ensure the global Enable Snapping toggle is turned on (located at the top right of the application window, or press % on your keyboard).
  2. Expand the snapping popover menu by clicking the arrow next to the snapping icon.
  3. Toggle on Snap to Guidelines.
  4. In the advanced snapping options, ensure Snap to guideline intersections is checked.
  5. Under the object snapping section, enable Snap an item's rotation center (or ensure miscellaneous points are allowed to snap).

Step 3: Reveal the Shape's Rotation Pivot

  1. Choose the Select and transform objects tool (S or F1).
  2. Click on the shape to select it.
  3. Click the shape a second time (do not double-click rapidly, as that opens path editing). The bounding box handles will change from resize arrows to rotation and skew arrows, and a small crosshair handle (+) will appear in the center of the shape. This crosshair is the rotation pivot.

Step 4: Move and Snap the Pivot

  1. Hover your cursor directly over the center crosshair handle until the cursor displays a hand or move icon.
  2. Click and drag the crosshair toward the intersection of your two guidelines.
  3. As the pivot approaches the crossing point, Inkscape will display a tooltip indicating "Guideline intersection" or "Intersection of guidelines," and the crosshair will snap magnetically into place.
  4. Release the mouse button.

The shape will now rotate precisely around the guideline intersection whenever you use the corner rotation handles.
