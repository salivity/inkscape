Tritanopia Simulation in Inkscape Using Display Filters

This guide explains how to view your vector artwork through a tritanopia color blindness simulation directly in Inkscape. By utilizing Inkscape's built-in non-destructive display filters, you can evaluate the visual accessibility of your designs for users with blue-yellow color vision deficiency without permanently altering your artwork's original color palette.

Step 1: Open the Display Filters Dialog

With your illustration open in Inkscape, navigate to the top menu bar and select View > Display filters.... This will open the Display Filters management window.

Step 2: Add the Color Blindness Filter

In the Display Filters window:

  1. Locate the available filters list on the left side.
  2. Select Color Blindness (or Color deficiency, depending on your Inkscape version).
  3. Click the Add button (represented by a right arrow or plus icon) to move it into the active filters list on the right.

Step 3: Select the Tritanopia Preset

  1. Click on the newly added Color Blindness entry in the active filters list to reveal its configuration settings below.
  2. Open the Type dropdown menu.
  3. Choose Tritanopia (blue-blind) from the list of available simulations.

Step 4: Enable the Simulation

Ensure that the checkbox next to the filter name in the active column is checked, and verify that the global Active or Enable toggle at the bottom of the dialog is selected. Your canvas will instantly update to simulate how the illustration appears to someone with tritanopia.

Step 5: Toggle or Remove the Filter

The display filter only alters your real-time viewport rendering; your underlying object colors remain untouched.

  • To toggle between the simulated view and normal view, check or uncheck the filter's box in the Display Filters dialog.
  • To exit the mode permanently, select the filter in the active column and click Remove, then close the dialog window.
