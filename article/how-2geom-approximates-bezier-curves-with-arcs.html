How 2Geom Approximates Bezier Curves with Arcs

The 2Geom library used by Inkscape converts Bezier curves into circular arc sequences by utilizing an adaptive biarc approximation algorithm. Because cubic Bezier curves have varying curvature that circular arcs cannot replicate directly, 2Geom breaks curves into segments at inflection points, computes two-arc sequences (biarcs) that match the boundary positions and tangents, checks the deviation against a user-defined tolerance, and recursively subdivides segments until the error falls within acceptable limits.

Inflection Point Splitting

Before fitting circular arcs, the cubic Bezier curve must be segmented into monotonic curvature sections. A single circular arc or biarc cannot represent an inflection point where the sign of the curvature changes. 2Geom calculates the roots of the cross product of the curve's first and second derivatives (\(C'(t) \times C''(t) = 0\)). The curve is split at these parameter values \(t\), guaranteeing that every resulting subsegment bends in a single direction without changing concavity.

Biarc Fitting

For each subsegment, 2Geom constructs a biarc. A biarc consists of two circular arcs sharing a common intermediate point and tangent, providing \(G^1\) (tangent) continuity across the entire span:

  1. Boundary Conditions: The algorithm extracts the start point \(P_0\), end point \(P_1\), and their corresponding unit tangent vectors \(T_0\) and \(T_1\) directly from the Bezier segment.
  2. Joint Point Selection: The connection point between the two arcs is computed based on geometric constraints. A common approach chosen in 2Geom is to place the joint point such that the ratio of the two arc radii minimizes curvature variation or equalizes the arc lengths.
  3. Center and Radius Computation: With the endpoints, tangents, and transition point established, the centers and radii of both circular segments are solved geometrically via the intersections of the normal lines.

Error Estimation

Once a candidate biarc is generated, 2Geom measures how closely it follows the original Bezier segment. The deviation is evaluated by calculating the maximum distance (Hausdorff distance approximation) between points on the Bezier curve and the corresponding points along the biarc:

  • Sample points along the parameter space \(t \in [0, 1]\) are projected onto the biarc.
  • The orthogonal distance from the Bezier curve to the arc surface is tested, particularly near the midpoint and points of maximum curvature deviation.

Recursive Subdivision

If the maximum deviation exceeds the specified tolerance threshold:

  1. The Bezier segment is subdivided into two smaller halves using de Casteljau's algorithm (typically at \(t = 0.5\) or at the parameter of maximum error).
  2. The biarc fitting process is applied recursively to both halves.
  3. If the computed error is within tolerance, the candidate biarc is accepted and appended to the output path.

This recursive reduction produces a sequence of smoothly connected circular arcs that accurately mimics the original Bezier path within the exact tolerance required for CNC machining, plotter export, or CAD interoperability.
