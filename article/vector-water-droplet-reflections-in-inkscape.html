Vector Water Droplet Reflections in Inkscape

This guide explains how to design realistic, complex vector water droplet reflections using Inkscape's path tools, gradient editors, and blur filters. By breaking down the anatomy of a liquid droplet into base drop shadows, internal light refractions, and sharp specular highlights, you can achieve a photorealistic three-dimensional effect directly within the software.

1. Create the Base Droplet Shape

Select the Draw Bezier curves and straight lines tool (or press B) to draw the main outline of the droplet. For a simple resting drop, draw an asymmetrical organic shape or an ellipse converted to a path (Path > Object to Path). Fine-tune the nodes using the Edit paths by nodes tool (N) to give it natural surface tension. Set the fill color to transparent or give it a very subtle, low-opacity tint matching the background surface.

2. Add the Base Cast Shadow

Water droplets cast a distinct, concentrated shadow because light passes through them rather than blocking it entirely:

  • Duplicate the base droplet path (Ctrl + D) and move it slightly downward and away from your intended light source.
  • Set the fill to black with an opacity of around 40% to 60%.
  • Go to the Fill and Stroke panel (Shift + Ctrl + F) and apply a slight Blur (typically 2% to 5%) to soften the edges.

3. Generate the Internal Refraction Gradient

Because water acts as a convex lens, light bends and creates brightness on the side opposite the light source:

  • Duplicate the base droplet shape again.
  • Select the Create and edit gradients tool (G) and apply a Radial Gradient.
  • Position the center of the gradient near the bottom edge (opposite your light source).
  • Set the inner stop to a bright, semi-transparent highlight color (such as light blue or white with 60% opacity) and the outer stop to a darker, more transparent tone (10% to 20% opacity). This creates the illusion of light pooling inside the bottom of the drop.

4. Build Secondary Internal Reflections

To create complex, realistic depth, add secondary reflections of the surrounding environment:

  • Draw a smaller, crescent-like path along the inner edge where the light enters the droplet.
  • Apply a Linear Gradient that fades from white to fully transparent.
  • For more realism, draw subtle, distorted geometric shapes inside the droplet to represent distorted reflections of objects or a horizon, setting their opacity between 10% and 30%.

5. Add Sharp Specular Highlights

Specular highlights define the glossy surface of the water:

  • Using the Circles and Ellipses tool (E), create a small white ellipse near the top edge closest to the light source.
  • Rotate and deform the shape slightly to match the curvature of the droplet.
  • Duplicate this highlight, make it smaller, and place it beside the first one to simulate a multi-point light source or window reflection.
  • Keep the main highlight pure white (#FFFFFF) with 90% to 100% opacity and apply a very low blur (0.5% to 1%) to keep the edge crisp.

6. Create the Caustic Glow

Water focuses light onto the surface directly beneath the shaded edge:

  • Draw a small, flattened ellipse on the ground plane directly under the bottom edge of the drop.
  • Fill it with pure white or a pale version of the background color.
  • Set the opacity to around 70% and apply a heavy Blur (10% to 20%) so it appears as a bright, focused spot of light resting inside the cast shadow.
