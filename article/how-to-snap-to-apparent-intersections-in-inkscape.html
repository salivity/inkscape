How to Snap to Apparent Intersections in Inkscape

This guide demonstrates how to snap lines or nodes to the virtual, apparent intersection of two non-intersecting, converging paths in Inkscape. While standard snapping targets physical overlaps, Inkscape includes dedicated snapping options and guide workflows designed to detect the exact projected meeting point of angled trajectories.

Method 1: Using Line Extension Snapping

Modern versions of Inkscape include built-in tracking for projected geometry, allowing you to snap directly to the projected intersection without modifying the original paths.

  1. Enable Global Snapping: Toggle the master snapping switch in the upper-right corner of the canvas (or press %).
  2. Open Snapping Preferences: Click the arrow next to the snapping magnet icon to open the full snapping popover, or open the dialog via View > Show/Hide > Snap Controls Bar.
  3. Activate Projection Snapping: Ensure that Snap to paths is active. Under the advanced alignment settings, enable Snap to line extensions (and ensure Snap to path intersections is also checked).
  4. Acquire the Projections: Select the Draw Bezier curves tool (B) or the Node tool (N). Hover your cursor along the first converging path to wake up its trajectory, then move toward the projected meeting area.
  5. Snap to the Point: A temporary extension guide will project from both lines. When your cursor reaches the point where both virtual lines cross, a tooltip reading "Intersection of line extensions" will appear. Click to place your node.

Method 2: Using Path-Aligned Guides

If the paths have complex node structures or line extension snapping fails to trigger, creating temporary guide intersections is the most precise alternative.

  1. Enable Guide Snapping: Open the Snapping popover and ensure that Snap to guides and Snap to guide intersections are toggled on.
  2. Create Aligned Guides:
    • Click and drag a guide from the ruler onto the first converging path.
    • Hold Shift while dragging the guide to rotate it until it snaps flush along the angle of the path.
    • Repeat the process for the second converging path.
  3. Draw to the Intersection: The two guides will automatically cross at the apparent intersection point. Select your drawing or transformation tool and click directly on the intersection of the two guides, which will highlight with an "X" snapping indicator.
  4. Clean Up: Delete the temporary guides by hovering over them and pressing Delete, or hide them via View > Guides.
