How Inkscape Calculates Polygon Centroids

Inkscape determines the geometric centroid, or center of mass, of an arbitrary non-symmetric polygon through exact analytical integration using its core computational geometry library, lib2geom. This article explains how the software converts the boundary path of an irregular shape into coordinates for its centroid using Green’s Theorem and the Shoelace method.

Boundary Representation and Green's Theorem

Rather than rasterizing a shape into pixels or using approximate mesh sampling, Inkscape treats polygons as ordered sequences of vector coordinates \((x_0, y_0), (x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_{n-1}, y_{n-1})\). To find the centroid of the enclosed area without manually dividing the polygon into disjoint triangles, Inkscape relies on Green’s Theorem.

Green’s Theorem converts a double integral over the interior area \(D\) of the polygon into a line integral evaluated strictly along its boundary curve \(C\):

\[\iint_D \left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right) dA = \oint_C (P\,dx + Q\,dy)\]

By carefully choosing functions \(P\) and \(Q\), the library computes the zeroth moment (the area) and the first moments (the balance points along the x and y axes) directly from the polygon's edges.

The Area and Coordinate Formulas

For a polygon defined by straight edges connecting consecutive vertices, this line integral simplifies to the classic surveyor's formula (or Shoelace formula).

First, the signed area \(A\) of the polygon is computed as:

\[A = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\]

Where \((x_n, y_n)\) wraps around to \((x_0, y_0)\). The sign of \(A\) indicates whether the vertices are oriented clockwise or counterclockwise.

Using this cross-product factor, denoted as \(w_i = (x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\), Inkscape calculates the coordinates of the centroid \((C_x, C_y)\) using the first area moments:

\[C_x = \frac{1}{6A} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (x_i + x_{i+1})(x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\]

\[C_y = \frac{1}{6A} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (y_i + y_{i+1})(x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\]

Geometric Principle: Signed Triangles

Geometrically, this calculation works by breaking the arbitrary polygon into signed triangles formed by each directed edge \((x_i, y_i) \to (x_{i+1}, y_{i+1})\) and an arbitrary origin \((0,0)\).

  1. For each edge, the term \((x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i)\) equals twice the signed area of that triangle.
  2. The centroid of a triangle with vertices at \((0,0)\), \((x_i, y_i)\), and \((x_{i+1}, y_{i+1})\) is located at \(\left(\frac{x_i + x_{i+1}}{3}, \frac{y_i + y_{i+1}}{3}\right)\).
  3. Multiplying each triangle's centroid by its respective signed area and dividing by the total area yields the weighted average position of all components.

Because the areas are signed, any regions outside the polygon cancel out automatically, allowing the formula to operate reliably on concave shapes, non-symmetric shapes, and shapes with arbitrary vertex distributions.

Handling Curved Edges

If the arbitrary polygon contains curved segments (such as Bézier curves), Inkscape’s lib2geom does not need to flatten the curves into simple lines. It applies the continuous form of Green’s Theorem directly to the parametric polynomial definitions of the Bézier segments, integrating the boundary polynomials analytically to ensure mathematically exact centroid placement.
