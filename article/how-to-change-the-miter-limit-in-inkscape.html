How to Change the Miter Limit in Inkscape

When designing vector paths with sharp angles in Inkscape, stroke corners can sometimes extend into excessively long, unwanted spikes. This visual glitch occurs because acute angles force miter joins outward beyond the intended boundary. This guide explains how to prevent these spikes by adjusting the miter limit threshold directly within Inkscape’s Stroke style settings, as well as alternative join styles to keep your stroke corners clean.

Why Stroke Spikes Occur

When a path uses a Miter join, Inkscape extends the outer edges of two meeting lines until they intersect, forming a sharp point. On acute corners (narrow angles), this intersection point shoots out significantly farther than the stroke's actual width.

To prevent infinite or disproportionate spikes, vector graphics use a Miter limit. This value defines the ratio between the length of the corner point and the stroke thickness. If the spike exceeds this threshold, Inkscape automatically converts the sharp tip into a flat, beveled corner.

Steps to Adjust the Miter Limit

  1. Select the Object: Click on the path or shape displaying the sharp corner spike using the Select tool (S or F1).
  2. Open the Fill and Stroke Dialog: Press Ctrl + Shift + F (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + F (macOS). Alternatively, go to the top menu and select Object > Fill and Stroke.
  3. Navigate to the Stroke Style Tab: At the top of the panel on the right, click the Stroke style tab.
  4. Locate the Miter Limit Field: Under the Join section, verify that the first icon (Miter join) is selected. Next to the join icons, find the numeric input labeled Miter limit.
  5. Lower the Value: Decrease the numeric value in the box.
    • The default value is typically 4.0.
    • Lowering the value toward 1.0 or 2.0 forces Inkscape to cut off spikes on wider angles.
    • Adjust the number until the sharp spike flattens into a neat bevel without affecting the corners you wish to keep sharp.

Alternative Solutions

If adjusting the miter threshold does not achieve the exact look you need, consider switching the corner style entirely in the same Join section:

  • Round Join: Replaces all sharp corners with smooth, circular arcs, completely eliminating pointy spikes while preserving stroke volume.
  • Bevel Join: Uniformly slices off all corners with flat edges, giving the path an angular, geometric appearance regardless of the angle's sharpness.
