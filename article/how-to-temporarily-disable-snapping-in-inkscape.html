How to Temporarily Disable Snapping in Inkscape

Inkscape features a robust snapping system designed to align objects to grids, guides, nodes, and bounding boxes with precision. However, active snapping can often interfere when you need fluid, freehand positioning. This guide explains the exact keyboard shortcut used to temporarily suspend snapping while actively moving or transforming elements on your canvas.

The Temporary Snapping Modifier

The keyboard modifier to temporarily disable snapping in Inkscape is the Shift key.

Holding Shift while dragging an object instantly overrides all active snap targets, allowing you to move, scale, or rotate the selection freely across the canvas without it jumping to nearby alignment points.

How to Use It Correctly

  1. Click and hold the mouse button on the object you want to move.
  2. Begin dragging the object.
  3. Press and hold the Shift key. Snapping will instantly deactivate.
  4. Drag the object to your desired position and release the left mouse button.
  5. Release the Shift key.

Note: It is important to begin dragging before holding the Shift key. Pressing Shift before clicking an object is the shortcut for adding or removing items from a selection, rather than disabling snapping.

Alternative: Global Snapping Toggle

If you need to disable snapping for an extended period rather than on a single movement, you can toggle the global snapping feature on and off:

  • Press the percent key (%) on your keyboard (often Shift + 5 depending on your keyboard layout).
  • Alternatively, click the Enable Snapping toggle button located in the top-right corner of the default Inkscape interface.
