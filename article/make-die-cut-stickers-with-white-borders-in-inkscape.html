Make Die-Cut Stickers with White Borders in Inkscape

Designing custom die-cut stickers requires creating a solid artwork layer, adding an expanded white border (the sticker margin), and establishing an exact cut path for a vinyl cutter or print shop. This guide walks you through the step-by-step process of preparing vector artwork, generating a clean white offset outline, and formatting a dedicated die-cut line using Inkscape.

Step 1: Prepare Your Vector Artwork

  1. Open Inkscape and import or design your vector graphic.
  2. Select all components of your graphic and group them by pressing Ctrl + G (or Cmd + G on macOS).
  3. Ensure your artwork has a transparent background.

Step 2: Create the Silhouette

To make a smooth outer border, you need a single solid shape behind the design.

  1. Select your grouped artwork and duplicate it by pressing Ctrl + D.
  2. With the duplicate selected, ungroup it completely by pressing Ctrl + Shift + G repeatedly until all sub-groups are broken.
  3. Convert all elements to paths by going to Path > Object to Path and Path > Stroke to Path.
  4. Merge everything into a single solid silhouette by selecting Path > Union.

Step 3: Add the White Border Offset

  1. Select your merged silhouette and open the Path Effects panel by navigating to Path > Path Effects.
  2. Click the + (Add Path Effect) button and choose Offset.
  3. In the Path Effects settings, choose Round for the Join style to ensure smooth sticker corners.
  4. Increase the Offset value until the border reaches your desired thickness (typically between 2 mm and 4 mm).
  5. Once satisfied, finalize the effect by selecting Path > Object to Path.
  6. Open the Fill and Stroke panel (Ctrl + Shift + F). Set the Fill to solid white (#FFFFFF) and set the Stroke paint to none.
  7. Lower the white shape behind your original graphic by pressing the Page Down key or clicking the Lower selection one step icon.

Step 4: Generate the Die-Cut Line

Print shops and cutting machines require a precise vector stroke to indicate where the blade should cut.

  1. Select your white border shape and duplicate it (Ctrl + D).
  2. If you want the cut line directly on the edge of the white border, keep the shape as is. If you want a bleed margin, apply another small outset.
  3. In the Fill and Stroke panel:
    • Set Fill to No paint (transparent).
    • Set Stroke paint to a distinct, high-contrast color (standard print-industry cut lines typically use 100% Magenta: R: 255, G: 0, B: 255).
    • Set the Stroke style width to a hairline thickness, such as 0.25 pt or 0.1 mm.
  4. Align the cut line directly over the white border using the Align and Distribute panel (Ctrl + Shift + A) to center both horizontally and vertically.

Step 5: Organize Layers and Export

  1. Open the Layers and Objects panel (Ctrl + Shift + L).
  2. Organize your file into distinct layers: place your original artwork on an "Artwork" layer, the white backing on a "White Underbase" layer, and the stroke path on a dedicated "CutContour" layer.
  3. Save your master file as an Inkscape SVG.
  4. Go to File > Save As and export the final production file as a PDF or EPS, ensuring that vector paths and transparency settings are preserved for printing.
