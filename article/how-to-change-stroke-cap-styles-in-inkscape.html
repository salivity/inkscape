How to Change Stroke Cap Styles in Inkscape

Stroke caps define the shape and behavior of the ends of open paths in vector design. In Inkscape, you can customize open path terminals using three primary styles: Butt, Round, and Square caps. Changing stroke cap styles allows you to control how paths terminate, whether you need precise geometric alignment or smooth, rounded curves for illustrations.

Opening the Fill and Stroke Dialog

To modify the stroke cap style of an object, you must first access the stroke properties panel:

  1. Select the path or line you want to edit using the Select Tool (S or F1).
  2. Open the Fill and Stroke panel by navigating to Object > Fill and Stroke from the top menu, or by pressing the keyboard shortcut Ctrl + Shift + F (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + F (macOS).
  3. In the panel that appears, click on the Stroke style tab.

Choosing a Stroke Cap Style

Within the Stroke style tab, locate the Cap section, which displays three distinct icons representing each available style:

  • Butt Cap (First Icon): Cuts the stroke off squarely right at the path's end node. The stroke does not extend past the endpoint coordinate, making it ideal for technical drawings and precise boundary alignment.
  • Round Cap (Second Icon): Adds a semicircular end extending past the path's end node. The radius of the rounded curve is equal to half the stroke width. This style is best suited for organic shapes, handwriting effects, and rounded UI elements.
  • Square Cap (Third Icon): Adds a rectangular extension that protrudes past the endpoint by a distance equal to half the stroke width. While it looks similar to the Butt cap, the extra length ensures corners and joints overlap cleanly without gaps when meeting perpendicular lines.

Click on any of the three icons to immediately apply the chosen cap style to your selected path.
