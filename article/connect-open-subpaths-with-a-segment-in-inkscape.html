Connect Open Subpaths with a Segment in Inkscape

Connecting two open subpaths with a straight segment in Inkscape allows you to bridge separate lines and create a single continuous vector shape. This guide explains the exact steps required to combine separate paths into one object and use the Node Tool to join two open endpoints with a new straight connecting line.

Step 1: Combine the Paths into One Object

If your two subpaths are currently separate objects, they must be combined into a single compound path before you can link their nodes.

  1. Select the Select and Transform objects tool (shortcut: S).
  2. Hold Shift and click both paths to select them simultaneously.
  3. Go to the top menu and select Path > Combine (or press Ctrl + K).

Step 2: Switch to the Node Tool

  1. Select the Edit paths by nodes tool from the left toolbar (shortcut: N).
  2. Click on the combined path to display all its nodes.

Step 3: Select the Two End Nodes

  1. Identify the two open endpoints from the different subpaths that you want to bridge.
  2. Hold Shift and click on both endpoint nodes, or click and drag a selection box around both nodes. When selected, the nodes will change color (typically turning blue or yellow).

Step 4: Join the Endnodes with a Segment

  1. Look at the Tool Controls Bar located horizontally across the top of the canvas.
  2. Click the Join selected endnodes with a new segment icon (represented by two separate square nodes connected by a line with an arrow).
  3. Inkscape will instantly draw a new straight path segment between the two selected nodes, merging the subpaths into a single uninterrupted path.
