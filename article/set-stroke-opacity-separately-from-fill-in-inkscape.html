Set Stroke Opacity Separately from Fill in Inkscape

In Inkscape, you can control the transparency of an object's outline without affecting its interior by utilizing individual color alpha channels rather than the global opacity slider. This guide explains how to use the Fill and Stroke dialog to set independent opacity levels for both the stroke and the fill of any vector shape.

Open the Fill and Stroke Dialog

Select the object you want to modify using the Select tool (S). Open the Fill and Stroke panel by pressing Ctrl + Shift + F (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + F (macOS), or by navigating to Object > Fill and Stroke in the top menu.

Avoid the Master Opacity Slider

At the bottom of the Fill and Stroke dialog, there is a slider labeled Opacity (%). Do not use this slider to adjust individual elements. This control acts as a master transparency setting for the entire object, reducing the visibility of the fill and the stroke simultaneously. Leave this value at 100%.

Adjust the Fill Opacity

  1. Click on the Fill tab at the top of the dialog.
  2. Ensure a flat color or gradient is selected.
  3. Locate the color mode sliders (such as RGB, HSL, or CMS).
  4. Find the A slider, which stands for Alpha (transparency).
  5. Drag the A slider to the left to increase transparency or to the right to increase opacity. Alternatively, enter a numeric value between 0 (completely invisible) and 100 (fully opaque).

Adjust the Stroke Opacity Independently

  1. Switch to the Stroke paint tab in the same dialog.
  2. Locate the color sliders corresponding to your stroke color.
  3. Find the A (Alpha) slider within this tab.
  4. Drag the slider to set your desired transparency for the stroke outline alone.

By adjusting the Alpha (A) value inside the Stroke paint tab independently from the Alpha value in the Fill tab, you achieve distinct transparency levels for the outline and the interior of your object.
