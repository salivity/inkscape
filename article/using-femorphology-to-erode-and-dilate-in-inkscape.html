Using feMorphology to Erode and Dilate in Inkscape

This guide explains how to use the SVG feMorphology filter primitive within Inkscape to erode (shrink) or dilate (expand) vector graphics. By taking advantage of Inkscape’s built-in Filter Editor, you can apply these non-destructive pixel-level transformations to paths, shapes, and editable text without permanently altering the underlying geometry.

Understanding feMorphology

The feMorphology filter primitive operates on the alpha channel or color channels of an element using two core operations:

  • Dilate: Expands the boundaries of an object, making lines thicker and shapes larger.
  • Erode: Thins the boundaries of an object, shrinking shapes and hollowing or removing thin details.

Unlike vector insets or outsets, feMorphology functions as a raster-level filter, making it fully reversible and compatible with live text.

Step 1: Open the Filter Editor

  1. Open your document in Inkscape and select the vector object you wish to modify.
  2. Open the Filter Editor panel by navigating to Filters > Filter Editor in the top menu.

Step 2: Create a New Filter

  1. In the Filter Editor panel, click the New button at the bottom of the left-hand column.
  2. A new filter entry (such as filter1) will appear. Double-click it to rename it to something descriptive, such as Morphology-Effect.

Step 3: Add and Connect the feMorphology Primitive

  1. Locate the Add Primitive drop-down menu at the bottom of the Filter Editor.
  2. Select Morphology from the list and click Add Primitive. A node labeled Morphology will appear in the central canvas of the panel.
  3. Connect the input: Drag the connection triangle from the left side of the Morphology primitive node to the Source Graphic column on the far left. This instructs the filter to use your original vector object as its input.

Step 4: Configure the Operator and Radius

  1. Click on the Morphology primitive in the list to reveal its Effect parameters at the bottom of the panel.
  2. Set the Operator:
    • Choose Dilate to expand the object's contours.
    • Choose Erode to contract or thin the object's contours.
  3. Adjust the Radius:
    • Use the Radius slider or input box to define the strength of the effect in pixels.
    • To apply uniform expansion or contraction, set identical values for the horizontal (X) and vertical (Y) radii. Adjusting them independently creates directional thinning or thickening.

Step 5: Apply the Filter to Your Graphic

  1. Select your target vector element on the canvas.
  2. In the Filter Editor panel, check the box next to the filter name you created.
  3. The selected graphic will immediately update to reflect the dilation or erosion effect.

To adjust the intensity later, select the object, return to the Filter Editor, and tweak the radius values. To remove the effect entirely, navigate to Filters > Remove Filters.
