How to Create a DTG White Underbase in Inkscape

Preparing artwork for direct-to-garment (DTG) printing on dark garments requires a solid white ink underbase beneath your colored design to ensure colors appear vibrant and opaque. This guide outlines the exact process for isolating your vector design, generating a dedicated underbase shape, applying an inset choke to prevent white edges from peeking out, and exporting the final production-ready file using Inkscape.

1. Prepare and Organize the Artwork

Ensure your artwork is fully finalized before creating the underbase:

  • Open your design in Inkscape and verify the document color profile and resolution.
  • Select all text and live shapes and convert them to vector paths by selecting Path > Object to Path.
  • If your design consists of multiple separate vector shapes, group them temporarily using Ctrl + G to keep them aligned.
  • Delete any background elements, guidelines, or mock-up shirt layers so only the printable artwork remains on a transparent canvas.

2. Duplicate the Design for the Underbase

Your underbase must match the exact dimensions and position of your color artwork:

  1. Open the Layers and Objects panel (Layer > Layers and Objects... or Ctrl + Shift + L).
  2. Create a new layer beneath your artwork layer and name it White Underbase.
  3. Select all visible artwork on your primary layer and press Ctrl + D to duplicate it.
  4. Move the duplicated artwork to the White Underbase layer by right-clicking and selecting Move to Layer..., or by dragging it within the Layers panel.
  5. Lock and hide the top color layer temporarily so you can focus entirely on the underbase.

3. Create a Solid Silhouette

The underbase needs to be a unified, solid shape covering every area where color ink will be deposited:

  1. Select all paths on the White Underbase layer.
  2. Ungroup the selection completely (Ctrl + Shift + G) until all elements are individual paths.
  3. Go to Path > Union (Ctrl + +) to merge overlapping paths into a single unified silhouette. If your artwork contains separate, non-touching elements, use Path > Combine (Ctrl + K).
  4. Remove any existing stroke outlines: in the Fill and Stroke panel (Ctrl + Shift + F), set Stroke paint to No paint.
  5. Set the Fill color. Most RIP software accepts pure white (#FFFFFF) or pure black (#000000) for the underbase mask. Check your specific printer/RIP software manual; some setups require a distinct spot color (such as 100% Magenta or a dedicated named swatch).

4. Apply a Choke (Inset)

Garment stretching and mechanical registration variance can cause the white underbase to visibly bleed past the edges of your colored ink. To prevent this, apply a "choke" by shrinking the underbase slightly:

  1. Select your merged underbase path.
  2. Go to Path > Inset (Ctrl + ().
  3. One or two insets (typically equivalent to 1–2 pixels, or roughly 0.5 to 1 pt) is standard for DTG printing.
  4. Fine-tune the inset step size under Edit > Preferences > Behavior > Steps > Inset/Outset by if you need precise fractional-millimeter adjustments.

5. Final Alignment and Export

  1. Make your top color layer visible again and unlock it.
  2. Zoom in closely to confirm that the white underbase sits completely inside the boundaries of the colored artwork.
  3. Save your master working file as an Inkscape SVG.
  4. Check your RIP software requirements for the production file:
    • Multi-layer PDF: Go to File > Save As... and choose Portable Document Format (*.pdf). Ensure layer data is retained if supported by your RIP.
    • Transparent PNG: If your RIP handles automated white generation from transparency, you may only need to export the top graphic. Otherwise, export the underbase as a separate grayscale 300 DPI PNG via File > Export..., selecting the Page or Selection area at 300 DPI. Ensure the export boundaries match the color pass identically.
