Create Seamless 2D Platformer Tilesets in Inkscape

Designing a 2D platformer tileset requires precision to ensure visual consistency and prevent visible seams between adjacent surfaces. This guide walks you through setting up Inkscape for pixel-perfect tile creation, designing core surface blocks, and constructing seamless 90-degree outer and inner corner tiles that align reliably in any game engine.

1. Document and Grid Setup

Accurate alignment depends entirely on grid snapping:

  1. Open Inkscape and go to File > Document Properties (Shift + Ctrl + D).
  2. Set the display units to px (pixels).
  3. Under the Grids tab, create a new Rectangular grid.
  4. Set Spacing X and Spacing Y to your target tile size (for example, 32.00000 px or 64.00000 px).
  5. Open the Snap Controls bar (usually on the right side). Enable snapping and activate Snap to grids, Snap bounding boxes, and Snap nodes, paths, and handles.

2. Creating the Core Flat Tiles

Before building corners, establish the flat surface textures:

  1. Center Fill Tile: Select the Rectangle tool (R) and draw a square snapping exactly to a single grid cell. Give it your base ground color and texture. Keep internal decorative elements at least 2 pixels away from the outer border, or ensure any detail crossing the left border enters at the exact same Y-coordinate on the right border.
  2. Top Surface Tile: Duplicate your fill tile and design the grass, stone, or surface lip along the top edge. Ensure the left and right edges match in color and thickness so they repeat infinitely across horizontal runs.

3. Designing the Outer 90-Degree Corners

Outer corners represent convex turns (such as the top-left or top-right edge of a floating platform):

  1. Base Block: Snap a new square to the grid adjacent to your top surface tile.
  2. Transfer Edges: Copy the vertical side profile of your wall tile to the outer vertical edge of this corner tile. Copy the horizontal top profile of your surface tile to the top horizontal edge.
  3. Resolve the Apex: Bring the horizontal and vertical details together at the outer vertex. For a sharp 90-degree look, allow the top surface border and the vertical side border to meet squarely at the top-left (or top-right) corner pixel. For a rounded look, use the Path > Inset or node-editing tools (N) to bevel the corner while keeping the connection points strictly aligned with the incoming horizontal and vertical edge heights.
  4. Fill the Remainder: Fill the lower-interior portion of the tile with the standard base fill color to match the surrounding ground tiles.

4. Designing the Inner 90-Degree Corners

Inner corners represent concave turns (such as where a vertical wall meets a flat floor in a cave or pit):

  1. Base Block: Draw a new square tile on the grid.
  2. Align the Meeting Edges: The inner corner contains ground on three quadrants and empty space (air) or wall face on only one.
  3. Forming the L-Shape:
    • On a bottom-right inner corner, the top surface profile runs across the upper half of the tile until it hits the vertical wall profile running down the right side.
    • Use the Pen tool (B) or boolean path operations (Path > Difference or Path > Union) to join the horizontal grass/lip edge with the vertical wall edge inside the tile.
  4. Maintain Boundary Continuity: The point where the inner corner lip touches the left edge must match the height of your standard top surface tile. The point where the vertical edge exits the bottom must match the width and position of your standard wall tile.

5. Testing for Seams and Artifacts

  1. Select your finished tiles and duplicate them (Ctrl + D).
  2. Arrange a 3x3 layout consisting of a top-left outer corner, top surface tiles, an inner corner, and bottom fill tiles.
  3. Zoom in to inspect the junction lines. If faint gaps or lines appear, verify that every tile's bounding box coordinates in the top toolbar are whole integers with no decimal values (e.g., X: 64.000, Y: 128.000, not 64.231).
  4. Group each finished tile individually (Ctrl + G) to keep components organized.

6. Exporting the Tileset

  1. Open the Export dialog (Shift + Ctrl + E).
  2. Switch to the Batch Export tab.
  3. Select each completed tile group.
  4. Set the export format to PNG and ensure the DPI is set to 96 (default standard 1:1 pixel ratio).
  5. Name your tiles systematically (e.g., wall_corner_outer_tl.png, wall_corner_inner_br.png) and click Export.
