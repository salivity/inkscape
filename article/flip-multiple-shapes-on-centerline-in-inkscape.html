Flip Multiple Shapes on Centerline in Inkscape

Flipping multiple shapes across their shared geometric centerline in Inkscape requires ensuring your bounding box calculation is set to geometric coordinates and using the selection flip tools. By either operating directly on the multi-selection or temporarily grouping the elements, Inkscape mirrors the entire collection across the common center point rather than reflecting each shape individually.

Step 1: Set the Bounding Box to Geometric

Inkscape calculates the center of objects using either a "Visual" bounding box (which includes stroke width) or a "Geometric" bounding box (which only accounts for the actual node paths). To flip precisely along the geometric centerline:

  1. Go to Edit > Preferences (or press Ctrl + Shift + P).
  2. In the left sidebar, click on Tools.
  3. Under Bounding box to use, select Geometric bounding box.
  4. Close the Preferences window.

Step 2: Select the Shapes

  1. Activate the Select and Transform Objects tool by pressing S or F1.
  2. Select all the shapes you want to flip by holding Shift and clicking each shape, or by clicking and dragging a selection box around all of them.

Step 3: Flip Across the Collective Centerline

By default, Inkscape flips multiple selected objects around the common center of the combined selection box:

  • To flip horizontally (across the collective vertical centerline): Press H on your keyboard, or click the Flip selected objects horizontally button on the top Tool Controls bar, or go to Object > Flip Horizontal.
  • To flip vertically (across the collective horizontal centerline): Press V on your keyboard, or click the Flip selected objects vertically button on the top Tool Controls bar, or go to Object > Flip Vertical.

Alternative: Grouping Before Flipping

If you want to guarantee that complex paths, clones, or grouped subgroups maintain their relative distances without accidental drift:

  1. Select all the shapes.
  2. Group them together by pressing Ctrl + G (or Object > Group). This creates a single compound boundary with a distinct geometric center.
  3. Press H to flip horizontally or V to flip vertically.
  4. Ungroup the shapes by pressing Ctrl + Shift + G (or Object > Ungroup) to return them to independent paths.
