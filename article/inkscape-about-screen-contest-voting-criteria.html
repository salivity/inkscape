Inkscape About Screen Contest Voting Criteria

Every major release of the open-source vector editor Inkscape features a new "About Screen" illustration selected through a public contest. The winning artwork is determined through a combination of strict technical qualification rules, community-driven voting mechanics, and subjective criteria regarding technical mastery and practical UI integration.

Technical Qualification Requirements

Before an artwork can be eligible for community voting, it must strictly adhere to the technical guidelines set by the Inkscape project:

  • Created in Inkscape: The entire artwork must be created exclusively using Inkscape.
  • Pure Vector Format: Submissions must be valid Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG) files without embedded raster images (such as PNGs or JPEGs), unless explicitly allowed under specific contest rules.
  • Typography and Paths: Any text included in the artwork must either be converted to paths or use open-source fonts that are publicly available without proprietary restrictions.
  • Open Licensing: Artists must agree to release their work under an open license, typically Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike (CC BY-SA) or compatible, allowing the Inkscape project to distribute and adapt the work.
  • Canvas Dimensions and Ratios: Artwork must match predefined aspect ratios and dimensions so it properly fits inside the software’s dialog window.

Community Voting Process

Once entries pass the initial moderation and technical check, the voting phase opens to the Inkscape community:

  • Voter Eligibility: To prevent vote manipulation and bot activity, voting is typically restricted to registered users on the official Inkscape website who meet specific activity thresholds, such as account age or community participation.
  • Scoring System: Inkscape often utilizes ranked-choice voting or multi-tier preference systems (such as the Schulze method or star ratings). Voters rank or rate their preferred entries rather than selecting a single favorite, ensuring the winner reflects a broad consensus rather than a polarized minority.

Core Voting Criteria

Voters evaluate submissions based on several essential artistic and functional factors:

Technical Vector Mastery

Voters look for artwork that showcases the depth and capabilities of Inkscape’s feature set. Successful entries often demonstrate sophisticated use of tools such as node editing, gradients, mesh gradients, path operations, live path effects (LPEs), and clipping masks.

Aesthetic Quality and Originality

The artwork serves as the introductory visual for the software, so artistic execution, color harmony, composition, and emotional resonance play a significant role in community scoring. Entries that present a clear, creative concept generally perform well.

Functional Composition for UI Integration

The About Screen must balance art with user interface requirements. Voters evaluate whether an entry leaves adequate, uncluttered negative space for the Inkscape logo, version number, credits, and interactive elements. Artwork that is too visually busy or obscures critical text is less likely to receive top marks.

Representation of the Project

Submissions that celebrate the values of the open-source community, creativity, and the spirit of digital freedom frequently resonate with the voter base. Themes often touch on growth, diversity, and innovation within digital arts.

Verification of the Winner

After the voting period closes, the top-voted entry undergoes a final source-file inspection by the Inkscape team. The project moderators verify that the underlying SVG elements were cleanly assembled, that no copyright-infringing assets were used, and that the file performs efficiently without causing software instability. Once approved, the artwork is officially merged into the codebase for the upcoming release.
