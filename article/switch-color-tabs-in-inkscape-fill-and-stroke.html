Switch Color Tabs in Inkscape Fill and Stroke

In Inkscape, you can customize how you select and adjust colors by switching between different color models—such as RGB, HSL, CMYK, and CMS—directly inside the Fill and Stroke dialog. This guide explains how to open the panel, access the flat color settings, and seamlessly switch between these color tabs to suit your project's workflow.

1. Open the Fill and Stroke Dialog

To view your color settings, first ensure the panel is visible:

  • Use the keyboard shortcut Shift + Ctrl + F (Windows/Linux) or Shift + Cmd + F (macOS).
  • Alternatively, go to the top menu and select Object > Fill and Stroke.

2. Select Your Target Paint Mode

At the top of the Fill and Stroke dialog, choose either the Fill tab or the Stroke paint tab, depending on which part of your object you want to adjust.

Directly beneath these tabs, select the Flat color icon (the solid square next to the "X" button). The color mode tabs will only appear when a flat color is active; they do not display for gradients, patterns, or unset paint.

3. Switch Between Color Tabs

Below the paint type icons, you will see a horizontal row of tabs labeled with different color models:

  • RGB: Adjusts Red, Green, and Blue values alongside Alpha (transparency).
  • HSL: Adjusts Hue, Saturation, and Lightness alongside Alpha.
  • CMYK: Provides Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Key (Black) sliders for print-oriented adjustments.
  • Wheel: Displays an HSL-based circular color picker with a triangle for saturation and value.
  • CMS: Color Management System tab, which allows you to apply colors defined by specific installed ICC color profiles.

Click directly on any of these labels to switch the active view. The sliders and numeric entry fields below will immediately update to match the chosen color space while retaining the current color value of your selected object.

Troubleshooting Hidden Tabs

If you do not see all the tabs (such as CMYK or CMS), the Fill and Stroke panel may be too narrow. Click and drag the left edge of the dock to widen the panel until all tabs become visible, or look for a small overflow arrow at the right end of the tab bar to access the hidden options.
