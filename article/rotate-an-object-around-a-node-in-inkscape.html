Rotate an Object Around a Node in Inkscape

Rotating an object around a specific point rather than its default center is essential for precise vector illustration in Inkscape. By adjusting the object's rotation center crosshair and utilizing Inkscape's snapping tools, you can easily anchor an object to any arbitrary node—whether on the object itself or another path—and rotate it with precision. This guide covers the exact steps required to configure your snapping settings, align the rotation center to a node, and perform the rotation.

Step 1: Enable Snapping for Nodes and Rotation Centers

To ensure the pivot point aligns perfectly with your chosen node, configure Inkscape’s snapping settings:

  1. Turn on the global snapping toggle by pressing % or clicking the magnet icon in the top-right corner.
  2. Open the snapping popover menu (the small arrow next to the magnet icon).
  3. Under the Nodes section, ensure that Node or Cusp node snapping is enabled.
  4. Under the Misc or Advanced section, enable Item's rotation center.

Step 2: Reveal the Rotation Center Crosshair

  1. Select the Select and transform objects tool from the left toolbar (shortcut: S or F1).
  2. Click the object you want to rotate. This displays the standard scale handles (straight arrows).
  3. Click the object a second time. The handles will switch to curved rotation arrows, and a small plus-shaped crosshair (+) will appear at the geometric center of the object. This crosshair is the object's rotation center.

Step 3: Move the Rotation Center to the Node

  1. Hover your cursor directly over the center crosshair until the cursor turns into a hand icon.
  2. Click and drag the crosshair toward the target node.
  3. Hover over the node until a snap indicator pop-up appears (such as "Rotation center to cusp node" or "Rotation center to smooth node").
  4. Release the mouse button. The rotation center is now locked directly onto the node.

Step 4: Rotate the Object

  1. Click and drag any of the curved corner arrows around the object. The object will now pivot strictly around the selected node.
  2. Hold down Ctrl while dragging to constrain the rotation to fixed 15-degree increments.
  3. For exact degree input, press Shift + Ctrl + M to open the Transform panel, switch to the Rotate tab, enter your desired angle, and click Apply.

How to Reset the Rotation Center

If you need to return the pivot point to the object's original geometric center:

  1. Click the object twice to make the rotation crosshair visible.
  2. Hold Shift and click directly on the crosshair. It will automatically snap back to the center of the bounding box.
