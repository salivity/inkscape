Select Objects Touching a Line in Inkscape

Inkscape provides a specialized touch selection mode that lets you select multiple objects simply by drawing a freehand line across them. Instead of dragging a traditional rectangular bounding box—which often captures unwanted background shapes—you can use a path-based line to precisely target only the elements you want. This guide explains how to use this feature with quick keyboard shortcuts and tool settings.

How to Use the Touch Selection Line

  1. Activate the Selector Tool: Click the Select and Transform Objects tool from the left toolbar, or press S (or F1) on your keyboard.
  2. Hold the Alt Key: Press and hold the Alt key on Windows/Linux, or Option on macOS.
  3. Draw the Selection Line: Click and drag your cursor across the canvas. A visible red line will trail behind your mouse pointer.
  4. Release to Select: Release the mouse button. Any object that touches or intersects the red line will instantly be selected.

Modifying an Existing Touch Selection

  • Add or Toggle Objects: Hold Shift + Alt (or Shift + Option on macOS) while clicking and dragging to draw another line. This adds newly touched objects to your current selection or toggles their selection state.
  • Combine with Box Selection: You can make an initial selection using standard click or box-drag methods, then use Shift + Alt to draw a line across additional shapes to include them.

When to Use Touch Selection

  • Complex Vector Artwork: Ideal for selecting specific curved paths, contour lines, or scattered nodes without accidentally selecting overlapping background layers.
  • Dense Layouts: Useful for weaving a path between closely packed objects where a rectangular marquee selection is too broad.
  • Linear Alignments: Fast for selecting rows or sequences of shapes arranged diagonally, vertically, or along irregular curves.
