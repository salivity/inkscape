Round Stroke Caps with Sharp Joins in Inkscape

This guide explains how to configure path stroke settings in Inkscape to create rounded stroke endpoints while preserving crisp, sharp corners at path vertices. By separating the Cap and Join properties inside the Fill and Stroke panel, you can independently control the appearance of open ends and corner intersections on any vector path.

The Required Settings

Inkscape decouples the styling of line endpoints from the styling of line intersections. To achieve rounded ends with sharp corners, you must configure two separate properties under the Stroke style tab:

  1. Cap: Round cap
  2. Join: Miter join

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Select the path you want to modify using the Select tool (S) or Node tool (N).
  2. Open the Fill and Stroke dialog by pressing Shift + Ctrl + F (or navigating to Object > Fill and Stroke in the top menu).
  3. Click on the Stroke style tab.
  4. Locate the Cap options (the row of three icons representing Butt, Round, and Square ends) and select the middle icon: Round cap. This rounds the open start and end nodes of your path.
  5. Locate the Join options directly below the Cap settings (representing Miter, Round, and Bevel corners) and select the first icon: Miter join. This forces all interior corners where line segments meet to remain sharp points.

Configuring the Miter Limit

When using a Miter join, acute angles can occasionally extend too far outward or automatically convert into flat bevels if the angle is too sharp.

Directly to the right of the Join options is the Miter limit input field. The default value is typically 4.0.

  • If your sharp corners are unexpectedly cutting off flat at sharp angles, increase the Miter limit value.
  • A higher value allows sharper, more acute angles to keep their pointed tip rather than clipping into a beveled corner.
