How to Put Stroke Behind Fill in Inkscape

By default, Inkscape centers an object's stroke along its path, causing the inner half of the stroke to overlap and obscure the fill. This article explains how to change the paint order directly within Inkscape's settings so that the stroke renders behind the fill. Using this native feature allows you to maintain thick outlines on text and shapes without shrinking the visible fill area or relying on duplicated layers.

Step 1: Open the Fill and Stroke Panel

Open your project in Inkscape and launch the formatting panel:

  • Go to Object in the top menu and select Fill and Stroke...
  • Alternatively, press the keyboard shortcut Ctrl + Shift + F (or Cmd + Shift + F on macOS).

Step 2: Select Your Object

Click on the shape, path, or text object whose stroke order you want to adjust using the Select and Transform Objects tool (shortcut: S). Ensure the object has both a visible fill and a stroke applied.

Step 3: Switch to the Stroke Style Tab

In the Fill and Stroke panel on the right side of your workspace, click the Stroke style tab. This tab contains controls for stroke width, dashes, corner joins, and paint ordering.

Step 4: Adjust the Paint Order

Locate the Order section near the bottom of the Stroke style tab. You will see three small icons representing different rendering layers:

  1. Stroke, Fill, Markers: The stroke sits behind the fill.
  2. Fill, Stroke, Markers: The default setting, where the stroke overlaps the fill.
  3. Fill, Markers, Stroke: The stroke is drawn on the very top.

Click the first icon—Stroke, Fill, Markers (represented by a square with the stroke drawn beneath the fill).

The Result

The fill will immediately render on top of the stroke. The inner half of the stroke is now hidden behind the fill, while the outer half remains visible along the exterior edge. If you need the outline to appear thicker, simply double the stroke width value in the Width field, as half of the stroke is now concealed beneath the fill.
