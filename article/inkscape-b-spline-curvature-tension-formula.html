Inkscape B-Spline Curvature Tension Formula

This article explains the underlying mathematics governing B-spline segments in Inkscape, detailing how the software calculates curve geometry, segment tension, and curvature. Inkscape represents B-splines primarily by translating uniform cubic B-splines into standard cubic Bézier curves using basis matrix transformations, while allowing local tension adjustments through rational weighting or knot multiplicity. Below are the exact formulas for the basis functions, conversion matrices, node tension adjustments, and the resulting curvature.

The Uniform Cubic B-Spline Formulation

Inkscape relies on the 2Geom geometry library to handle path math. Standard B-spline paths without custom node weights are calculated as uniform cubic B-splines (\(p = 3\)). A segment \(S_i(t)\) defined over the parameter interval \(t \in [0, 1]\) by four consecutive control points \(P_{i-1}, P_i, P_{i+1}, P_{i+2}\) is governed by the following matrix equation:

\[S_i(t) = \frac{1}{6} \begin{bmatrix} t^3 & t^2 & t & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \\ 3 & -6 & 3 & 0 \\ -3 & 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} P_{i-1} \\ P_i \\ P_{i+1} \\ P_{i+2} \end{bmatrix}\]

This evaluates to the summation of four cubic blending functions:

\[S_i(t) = b_0(t)P_{i-1} + b_1(t)P_i + b_2(t)P_{i+1} + b_3(t)P_{i+2}\]

where:

  • \(b_0(t) = \frac{1}{6}(1 - t)^3\)
  • \(b_1(t) = \frac{1}{6}(3t^3 - 6t^2 + 4)\)
  • \(b_2(t) = \frac{1}{6}(-3t^3 + 3t^2 + 3t + 1)\)
  • \(b_3(t) = \frac{1}{6}t^3\)

Conversion to SVG Cubic Bézier Segments

Because the SVG specification does not have a native B-spline element, Inkscape converts each B-spline segment into an equivalent cubic Bézier segment defined by control points \(B_0, B_1, B_2, B_3\). The conversion equations applied are:

\[B_0 = \frac{1}{6}P_{i-1} + \frac{2}{3}P_i + \frac{1}{6}P_{i+1}\] \[B_1 = \frac{2}{3}P_i + \frac{1}{3}P_{i+1}\] \[B_2 = \frac{1}{3}P_i + \frac{2}{3}P_{i+1}\] \[B_3 = \frac{1}{6}P_i + \frac{2}{3}P_{i+1} + \frac{1}{6}P_{i+2}\]

Tension and Node Weighting

In Inkscape's B-spline Live Path Effect (LPE), tension can be modified per node. This behavior is modeled via Non-Uniform Rational B-Splines (NURBS) or knot multiplicity:

  1. Rational Weighting: A weight \(w_i \ge 0\) is assigned to each control point. When weights deviate from 1, the curve segment is evaluated as:

\[C(t) = \frac{\sum_{j=0}^{3} w_{i+j-1} b_j(t) P_{i+j-1}}{\sum_{j=0}^{3} w_{i+j-1} b_j(t)}\]

Increasing \(w_i \to \infty\) pulls the curve closer to \(P_i\), increasing the local tension and tightening the bend. Setting \(w_i = 0\) reduces the influence of that point.

  1. Knot Multiplicity (Sharp Corners): To create sharp corners (maximum tension), Inkscape duplicates knots in the knot vector. Repeating a knot three times (\(k = 3\)) reduces local continuity from \(C^2\) to \(C^0\), forcing the curve directly through the control point:

\[C(t_k) = P_i\]

Curvature Formula (\(\kappa\))

The curvature \(\kappa\) at any point \(t\) on the segment measures the rate of change of the tangent direction with respect to arc length. For a parametric curve \(S(t) = (x(t), y(t))\), curvature is determined by:

\[\kappa(t) = \frac{|x'(t)y''(t) - y'(t)x''(t)|}{(x'(t)^2 + y'(t)^2)^{3/2}}\]

The first and second derivatives are obtained directly from the cubic polynomial basis:

\[S'(t) = \frac{1}{6} \begin{bmatrix} 3t^2 & 2t & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \\ 3 & -6 & 3 & 0 \\ -3 & 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{P}\]

\[S''(t) = \frac{1}{6} \begin{bmatrix} 6t & 2 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 3 & -3 & 1 \\ 3 & -6 & 3 & 0 \\ -3 & 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{P}\]

where \(\mathbf{P}\) is the column vector \([P_{i-1}, P_i, P_{i+1}, P_{i+2}]^T\). Substituting \(S'(t)\) and \(S''(t)\) into the curvature equation yields the instantaneous curvature value across any point on the segment.
