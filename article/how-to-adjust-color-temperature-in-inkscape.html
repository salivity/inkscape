How to Adjust Color Temperature in Inkscape

Adjusting the color temperature of an entire vector illustration in Inkscape allows you to quickly alter the mood of your artwork, shifting it toward warm sunlight tones or cool, shadowed hues. Instead of manually editing individual fills and strokes, you can use Inkscape's built-in Color extensions to modify the color balance of your entire composition at once. This guide covers how to prepare your artwork and use extensions like RGB Adjust and HSL Adjust to achieve a consistent color temperature shift.

Step 1: Select Your Illustration

Before applying any color extension, you must select the vector elements you wish to modify.

  1. Open your project in Inkscape.
  2. Press Ctrl + A (or Cmd + A on macOS) to select all elements in the canvas, or drag a selection box around the entire illustration.
  3. While Inkscape extensions can process objects nested inside groups, complex multi-level groups or clipped objects can occasionally cause processing errors. If you experience issues, consider selecting all elements and ungrouping them by pressing Ctrl + Shift + G repeatedly until all groups are unpacked.

Note: Inkscape’s color extensions primarily affect flat fills and strokes. Gradients, patterns, and embedded raster images will not be altered.

Step 2: Use the RGB Adjust Extension for Precise Temperature Control

The most direct way to simulate optical color temperature is through the RGB channels. Warmer temperatures introduce more red and yellow light, while cooler temperatures introduce blue light.

  1. Go to the top menu and select Extensions > Color > RGB Adjust...
  2. Check the Live preview box in the bottom-left corner of the dialog to see your adjustments in real time.
  3. Adjust the channel sliders according to your desired temperature:
    • To warm up the illustration:
      • Increase the Red (%) slider (e.g., +10% to +20%).
      • Keep the Green (%) slider neutral or add a slight positive value (+2% to +5%) to introduce warm yellow tones.
      • Decrease the Blue (%) slider (e.g., -5% to -15%).
    • To cool down the illustration:
      • Increase the Blue (%) slider (e.g., +10% to +20%).
      • Decrease the Red (%) slider (e.g., -10% to -20%).
      • Keep the Green (%) neutral or slightly reduced.
  4. Click Apply to make the changes permanent, then close the dialog.

Step 3: Alternative Method Using HSL Adjust

If you prefer shifting colors collectively along the color wheel rather than balancing individual channels, the HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness) extension is an alternative.

  1. Select your illustration.
  2. Navigate to Extensions > Color > HSL Adjust...
  3. Check Live preview.
  4. Adjust the Hue (°) slider by small increments (typically between -10° and +10°). Shifting the hue rotates all colors on the spectrum, which can simulate warm or cool ambient lighting across an entire palette.
  5. Optionally increase or decrease Saturation (%) to enhance the intensity of warm sunsets or dull the palette for a cold, winter atmosphere.
  6. Click Apply when satisfied.

Troubleshooting and Best Practices

  • Save a Backup: Color extensions modify vector properties directly and cannot always be perfectly reversed with standard undo commands if the document has been saved and closed. Always save a copy of your original file before applying global color transformations.
  • Gradients: If your artwork relies heavily on linear or radial gradients, edit their stops using the Gradient Tool (G) or convert key sections to flat colors before running the extension, as the Color extensions will skip gradient definitions.
