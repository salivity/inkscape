How to Rapidly Duplicate Objects in Inkscape

Inkscape includes a fast, interactive duplication feature known as "spacebar stamping" that lets you drop copies of an object continuously while moving, rotating, or scaling it across the canvas. This guide explains how to activate the spacebar stamp, how to combine it with other transformation tools, and how to use modifier keys to streamline your vector workflow.

Step-by-Step: Using the Spacebar Stamp

  1. Select the Object: Activate the Select and Transform Objects tool (press S or F1) and click on the object, path, or group you want to duplicate.
  2. Click and Drag: Click and hold the left mouse button on the object to start moving it across the canvas.
  3. Press the Spacebar: While keeping the left mouse button held down, tap the Spacebar whenever you want to place a copy. Each tap leaves an exact duplicate (a "stamp") at the current cursor position.
  4. Finish the Action: Release the left mouse button when you reach the final position for the original object.

Stamping During Other Transformations

The spacebar stamping technique is not limited to simple movement. You can stamp copies during any interactive transformation:

  • Rotation: Click the object a second time to reveal the rotation handles. Click and drag a corner handle to rotate, then tap the Spacebar to stamp copies at various angles around the rotation center.
  • Scaling: Click and drag any of the bounding box resize handles, tapping the Spacebar to leave copies at progressively larger or smaller sizes.
  • Skewing: Drag the side handles to skew the object and press the Spacebar to create dynamic sheared sequences.

Using Modifier Keys with Stamping

You can combine standard Inkscape modifier keys with the spacebar stamp to maintain precision:

  • Ctrl: Constrains movement to strict horizontal or vertical axes, or constrains rotation to 15-degree increments.
  • Shift: Scales objects symmetrically from the center or rotates around the opposite corner instead of the center point.
  • Alt: Allows freehand movement with pixel-based movement constraints.

Key Benefits

Unlike the standard duplicate shortcut (Ctrl + D), which places copies directly on top of the original, spacebar stamping eliminates the need to duplicate and reposition repeatedly. It is particularly useful for creating patterns, foliage, motion trails, scatter effects, and complex geometric layouts in seconds.
