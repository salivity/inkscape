Select Multiple Nested Objects in Inkscape Layers

This guide explains how to use Inkscape's Layers and Objects dialog to select multiple non-contiguous elements buried inside different nested groups. By utilizing the tree view and standard modifier keys, you can isolate and manipulate specific vector elements simultaneously without the need to ungroup your artwork or click through complex canvas layers.

Step 1: Open the Layers and Objects Dialog

To view the full hierarchy of your document, you need the combined Layers and Objects panel visible:

  • Go to the top menu and select Layer > Layers and Objects... or press the keyboard shortcut Ctrl + Shift + L (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + L (macOS).

Step 2: Expand the Nested Groups

In the Layers and Objects panel, groups appear as folder-like structures with arrow icons next to their names.

  1. Locate the parent group containing your desired elements.
  2. Click the small triangular arrow next to the group name to reveal its contents.
  3. Repeat this process for any subgroups to fully expose the individual paths, shapes, or text objects you intend to select.

Step 3: Make Non-Contiguous Selections

Once the nested elements are visible in the panel tree, use the non-contiguous selection shortcut:

  1. Click the first nested object in the panel to highlight it.
  2. Press and hold the Ctrl key (or Cmd on macOS).
  3. While holding the key, click any other nested object in the list, regardless of whether it resides in the same group or inside an entirely different group branch.
  4. Release the key once all desired objects are highlighted.

Key Considerations

  • Avoid the Canvas for Mixed Nested Selections: Clicking objects directly on the canvas can trigger group isolation or accidentally select the bounding parent group. Selecting directly from the Layers and Objects list bypasses the visual stacking order entirely.
  • Locked or Hidden Items: Ensure none of the target objects or their parent groups have the padlock (lock) or eye (hide) icons toggled on, as locked or hidden items cannot be selected.
  • Transforming Selection: Any transformations (such as moving, scaling, or changing fill color) applied while these non-contiguous objects are highlighted will affect only the selected sub-elements, leaving the surrounding group structures intact.
