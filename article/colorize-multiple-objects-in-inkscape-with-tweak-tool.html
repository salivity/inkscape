Colorize Multiple Objects in Inkscape with Tweak Tool

The Tweak tool in Inkscape offers a dynamic way to paint colors smoothly across multiple vector objects at once, eliminating the need to adjust individual fill properties manually. This guide covers how to set up your artwork, configure the Tweak tool for color blending, and apply soft gradients and color transitions across arrays of shapes.

1. Prepare and Select Your Objects

The Tweak tool only affects active selections.

  1. Create or arrange the shapes you want to colorize (such as a grid of circles, polygons, or scattered shapes).
  2. Use the Select tool (S) or press Ctrl + A to select all the objects you want to include in the colorizing process.

2. Activate the Tweak Tool

  1. Select the Tweak objects by sculpting or painting tool from the left toolbar, or press the shortcut key W.
  2. Look at the top Tool Controls bar to adjust your brush settings:
    • Width: Set the diameter of the brush influence area.
    • Force: Set the strength of the effect. A lower force (e.g., 10 to 25) ensures a gradual, smooth color transition.

3. Choose the Color Painting Mode

In the Tool Controls bar at the top, locate the color-related modes:

  • Paint: Click the icon that looks like a paintbrush over squares (Paint the fill of selected objects with current color).
  • Choose whether you want the color change to affect the Fill, the Stroke, or both by toggling the adjacent channel buttons.

4. Apply Colors Smoothly

  1. Click any color from the bottom color palette to set your active color.
  2. Click and drag your cursor over the selected objects. The active color will blend into the existing fill colors of the objects beneath the brush.
  3. Select a new color from the palette and brush over adjacent areas to blend different hues seamlessly across the canvas.
  4. Apply multiple light strokes rather than holding down the mouse in one spot to maintain smooth gradients.

5. Blend and Smooth Existing Colors

If your transitions look too sharp, you can blend the colors further:

  1. In the Tool Controls bar, switch the mode to Smooth colors on selected objects.
  2. Drag the brush over the borders between different colored objects. This averages the colors in the brush radius, producing a smooth, unified gradient effect across the entire group.
