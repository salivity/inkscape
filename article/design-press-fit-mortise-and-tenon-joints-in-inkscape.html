Design Press-Fit Mortise and Tenon Joints in Inkscape

Designing press-fit mortise and tenon joints in Inkscape requires precise vector construction and accurate compensation for machine kerf. This guide covers how to configure Inkscape's measurement settings, accurately measure your raw material, calculate kerf offsets, and utilize shape tools and boolean operations to produce interlocking tabs and slots that fit tightly without glue.

1. Configure Inkscape Measurement Settings

Before drawing your joints, ensure Inkscape measures the actual geometry of your paths rather than the visual stroke width.

  1. Go to Edit > Preferences > Tools.
  2. Select Geometric bounding box instead of "Visual bounding box." This ensures dimensions entered in the toolbar reflect the cut line, ignoring stroke thickness.
  3. Change your global display units to millimeters or inches via File > Document Properties > Display units.

2. Measure Material and Kerf

A true press fit relies on sub-millimeter accuracy.

  1. Measure Material Thickness: Use digital calipers to measure your stock in multiple places. Sheet materials like plywood and acrylic rarely match their nominal dimensions (e.g., "3mm" plywood often measures 2.8mm to 3.2mm).
  2. Determine Beam Kerf: Laser cutters and CNC endmills remove material along the path. A typical CO2 laser beam removes roughly 0.1mm to 0.2mm of material (kerf).
  3. Calculate Joint Tolerances: For a friction or press fit, the mortise (hole) must be slightly smaller than the tenon (tab).
    • Mortise Width: Material Thickness minus Kerf.
    • Tenon Width: Desired joint length plus Kerf (or keep the tenon nominal and reduce the mortise dimension).

3. Draw the Tenon (Tab)

  1. Select the Rectangle tool (R).
  2. In the top toolbar, set the exact width and height:
    • Width: Your desired joint width (e.g., 20.0 mm).
    • Height: The exact measured thickness of the mating material.
  3. Align the tenon to the edge of your workpiece using the Align and Distribute panel (Ctrl+Shift+A).
  4. Select both the base part and the tenon rectangle, then navigate to Path > Union (Ctrl++) to merge them into a single continuous outline.

4. Draw the Mortise (Slot)

  1. Select the Rectangle tool (R).
  2. Enter the dimensions adjusted for kerf:
    • Width: Match the tenon width, reduced by your kerf tolerance (e.g., 19.85 mm for a snug fit).
    • Height: Measured material thickness reduced by the laser kerf (e.g., if material is 3.0 mm and kerf is 0.15 mm, set height to 2.85 mm).
  3. Position the rectangle on the mating part using Align and Distribute.
  4. If the slot is internal, select the main piece and the slot rectangle, then click Path > Difference (Ctrl+-) to cut the opening through the path.

5. Add Friction Bumps for Reliable Press Fits

Wood expansion and manufacturing variances can cause straight press-fit slots to fail or loosen over time. Adding small interference bumps ensures a consistent lock.

  1. Convert the mortise rectangle to a path via Path > Object to Path (Ctrl+Shift+C).
  2. Select the Edit paths by nodes tool (N).
  3. Double-click the inside edge of the mortise slot to create additional nodes.
  4. Pull the center node inward by 0.05 mm to 0.1 mm to create a subtle triangular or rounded ridge along the slot wall. This ridge will compress upon insertion, securing the joint firmly regardless of minor material variations.
