How Inkscape Manages Donations Through the SFC

Inkscape manages its community donations through a fiscal sponsorship agreement with the Software Freedom Conservancy (SFC), a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization. Under this arrangement, the SFC handles the legal, financial, and administrative overhead of processing donations, while the Inkscape Project Leadership Committee (PLC) maintains full governing authority over how the funds are budgeted and spent. This model ensures that financial contributions directly advance Inkscape's development while complying with non-profit regulations.

The Role of the Software Freedom Conservancy

The Software Freedom Conservancy acts as Inkscape's legal and financial home. Because open-source projects rarely have their own incorporated corporate entities, the SFC provides the necessary infrastructure to accept donations legally. When a user contributes money to Inkscape, the transaction is processed and held by the SFC in an account earmarked specifically for the Inkscape project.

This partnership provides several key operational benefits:

  • Tax Deductibility: In jurisdictions like the United States, donations made through the SFC are tax-deductible for donors.
  • Compliance and Accounting: The SFC handles tax filings, payroll compliance, and financial audits, shielding developers from administrative burdens.
  • Legal Protection: The SFC can hold project assets, such as trademarks and domain names, in trust for the public benefit.

Governance by the Inkscape Leadership Committee

Although the SFC holds the funds, it does not unilaterally decide how Inkscape's money is used. Spending decisions rest entirely with the Inkscape Project Leadership Committee (PLC), which functions as the project's board of directors.

The PLC is composed of elected community members and contributors. When the project needs to make an expenditure, the PLC votes on proposals. Once a budget or expenditure is approved internally, the PLC submits a formal disbursement request to the SFC, which verifies that the expense aligns with non-profit rules and executes the payment.

How Donated Funds Are Allocated

The Inkscape board directs community donations toward initiatives that directly sustain and accelerate the software's growth:

  • Contracted Development: Funds are used to hire developers for specific goals, such as addressing complex technical debt, accelerating major release cycles, or implementing critical new features.
  • Hackathons and Contributor Travel: The board funds in-person hackathons and reimburses travel expenses for contributors who attend workshops, developer sprints, and open-source conferences.
  • Infrastructure and Hardware: Donations cover hosting fees, continuous integration (CI) infrastructure, testing hardware (such as drawing tablets and multi-platform machines), and developer tooling.
  • Bounties and Bug Fixes: The project periodically funds targeted initiatives to resolve high-priority bugs, optimize performance, and improve documentation.

Through this collaborative framework, the Inkscape board ensures that community donations are spent transparently, ethically, and effectively to support the long-term health of the software.
