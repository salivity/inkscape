Apply Gradient Fill to Editable Text in Inkscape

Applying a gradient fill across text in Inkscape does not require converting the letters into outlines or path shapes. By using the Fill and Stroke dialog alongside the Gradient tool, you can apply smooth linear or radial color transitions across an entire line of text while keeping the text completely editable. This guide walks you through selecting your text, applying the gradient, and adjusting color stops directly on the canvas.

1. Create or Select Your Text

  1. Select the Text tool (shortcut: T) from the left toolbar.
  2. Click on the canvas and type your text.
  3. Keep the text as an active text object—do not use the "Path > Object to Path" command, as this will destroy the editable nature of your font.

2. Open the Fill and Stroke Panel

  1. Select your text object using either the Select tool (S) or the Text tool.
  2. Open the Fill and Stroke panel by pressing Ctrl + Shift + F (or Cmd + Shift + F on macOS), or go to Object > Fill and Stroke in the top menu.
  3. Click on the Fill tab at the top of the panel.

3. Choose the Gradient Type

Inside the Fill tab, click one of the gradient buttons located just below the tabs:

  • Linear gradient: Creates a straight-line transition between colors.
  • Radial gradient: Creates a circular transition radiating outward from a center point.

Once clicked, Inkscape applies a default two-stop gradient across the bounding box of your text object.

4. Adjust the Gradient Handles on the Canvas

  1. Switch to the Gradient tool by pressing G on your keyboard or selecting it from the left toolbar.
  2. You will see a gradient line appear directly over your text with handles at each end (a square handle for the start point and a circle handle for the end point).
  3. Click and drag the square handle to position the beginning of the gradient.
  4. Click and drag the circle handle to position the end of the gradient. You can extend these handles far past the left and right edges of the text to control how gradually the colors blend.

5. Modify Colors and Add Color Stops

  • Change Stop Colors: Click directly on either the square or circle handle, then select a color from the color palette at the bottom of the screen, or choose an exact color inside the Fill and Stroke panel.
  • Add More Colors: Double-click anywhere along the gradient line connecting the two handles to create a new color stop (represented by a diamond). Select this new stop and assign it a color from the palette.
  • Delete Stops: Click an existing stop to select it and press the Delete key.

Because the text remains a native text object, you can switch back to the Text tool at any time to edit the spelling, change the font, or adjust letter spacing; the gradient will automatically adapt across the updated characters.
