Create Unequal Panel Tri-Fold Brochures in Inkscape

Designing a professional tri-fold brochure requires accounting for paper thickness during folding, which means the inner panel must be slightly narrower than the front and back panels to prevent paper buckling. This guide explains how to calculate correct panel widths, configure document dimensions and bleeds, set precise folding guides, and structure your layout across multi-page spreads using Inkscape.

Understanding Tri-Fold Panel Dimensions

A standard letter-size tri-fold brochure (11 × 8.5 inches) folded in a traditional "C-fold" consists of three panels per side: the front cover, the back cover, and the inside tuck-in flap. Because the tuck-in flap folds inside the other two panels, it must be shortened by 1/16 inch (0.0625 inches) to 1/8 inch (0.125 inches).

For an 11-inch wide document with a 1/16-inch compensation:

  • Two Standard Panels: 3.6875 inches each (3 11/16 inches)
  • One Short Panel (Tuck Flap): 3.625 inches (3 5/8 inches)
  • Total Width: 3.6875 + 3.6875 + 3.625 = 11.0 inches

For A4 paper (297 × 210 mm):

  • Two Standard Panels: 100 mm each
  • One Short Panel: 97 mm
  • Total Width: 100 + 100 + 97 = 297 mm

Document and Bleed Setup

  1. Open Inkscape and press Ctrl+Shift+D to open the Document Properties dialog.
  2. Under the Page tab, set Display units to in (inches) or mm (millimeters).
  3. Set Format to your target paper size (e.g., US Letter or A4) and ensure the orientation is set to Landscape.
  4. In the Bleeds section, enter 0.125 in (or 3 mm) for Top, Bottom, Left, and Right to allow artwork to extend past the trim edge.
  5. In Inkscape 1.2 or later, use the Create and Edit Document Pages tool (Shift+P) in the left toolbar to add a second page. Page 1 will serve as the Outside Spread, and Page 2 will serve as the Inside Spread.

Panel Orientation Across Spreads

Because the outside and inside of the brochure mirror each other, the narrow panel shifts position depending on the spread:

  • Outside Spread (Page 1):
    • Left: Inside Flap (Short: 3.625 in / 97 mm)
    • Middle: Back Cover (Standard: 3.6875 in / 100 mm)
    • Right: Front Cover (Standard: 3.6875 in / 100 mm)
  • Inside Spread (Page 2):
    • Left: Inside Left Panel (Standard: 3.6875 in / 100 mm)
    • Middle: Inside Center Panel (Standard: 3.6875 in / 100 mm)
    • Right: Inside Flap Reverse (Short: 3.625 in / 97 mm)

Creating Accurate Fold Guides

Using precise X-coordinates for vertical guides ensures exact fold lines.

Setting Guides for Page 1 (Outside Spread)

  1. Drag a vertical guide from the left ruler onto the canvas.
  2. Double-click the guide to open its properties.
  3. Set the X coordinate for the first fold line to 3.625 in (or 97 mm). Click OK.
  4. Drag a second vertical guide onto the canvas.
  5. Double-click it and set the X coordinate to 7.3125 in (3.625 + 3.6875) or 197 mm (97 + 100). Click OK.

Setting Guides for Page 2 (Inside Spread)

  1. Navigate to Page 2.
  2. Set the first vertical fold guide at X coordinate 3.6875 in (or 100 mm).
  3. Set the second vertical fold guide at X coordinate 7.375 in (3.6875 + 3.6875) or 200 mm (100 + 100).

Adding Safe Margin Guides

Content such as text and critical graphics should sit inside a "safe zone" at least 0.125 to 0.25 inches (3 to 6 mm) away from trim edges and fold lines:

  1. Place additional vertical guides 0.125 inches to the left and right of each fold guide.
  2. Place horizontal and vertical guides 0.125 inches inward from the four page boundaries.
  3. To lock guides and prevent accidental movement during layout design, go to Document Properties and check Lock all guides, or hover over the ruler intersection and toggle the lock icon.

Designing and Exporting

  1. Use separate layers (Ctrl+Shift+L) for background graphics, text, and technical markings to keep the workspace organized.
  2. Extend all background colors and imagery across the red bleed boundary to prevent white edges after commercial trimming.
  3. Keep important copy, headlines, and logos strictly within the safe margin guides.
  4. When exporting for commercial printing, choose File > Save As... and select Portable Document Format (*.pdf). Ensure that Use exported object's size is selected and bleeds are included according to your printer's specifications.
