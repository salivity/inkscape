How to Create Inset Shadow Outlines in Inkscape

Creating inset shadow outlines adds depth and dimension to cartoon illustrations while maintaining clean, crisp vector borders. This guide covers the complete process of generating internal shadow shapes in Inkscape using path offsets, boolean operations, and clipping masks. By following these direct steps, you will learn how to produce consistent, stylized shadows that sit neatly inside your cartoon character designs and line art without spilling over your outlines.

Method 1: The Duplicate and Difference Technique

This is the fastest and most common method for creating simple crescent-style inset shadows along one side of an object.

  1. Select the Base Shape: Click on the colored fill shape where you want the shadow to appear (beneath your black outline layer).
  2. Duplicate the Shape: Press Ctrl + D to create a duplicate directly on top of the original.
  3. Move the Duplicate: Use the arrow keys or drag the duplicate slightly in the direction of your light source.
  4. Duplicate the Original Again: Click the original base shape below and press Ctrl + D once more.
  5. Subtract to Create the Shadow: Select the topmost duplicate and the second duplicate you just made, then go to Path > Difference (or press Ctrl + -). This creates a thin crescent shape along the opposite edge of your light source.
  6. Style the Shadow: Change the color of this new shape to a darker shade of your base color or black with reduced opacity (e.g., 20% to 40% alpha) to create a subtle inset tone.

Method 2: The Inset Path Command

If you want a uniform shadow border that follows the entire inner contour of a shape, use the built-in Inset tool.

  1. Duplicate the Base Path: Select your shape and press Ctrl + D.
  2. Apply Inset: Go to Path > Inset (shortcut: Ctrl + (). Repeat this shortcut multiple times until the inner shape reaches the desired thickness.
    • Tip: You can adjust the inset step size under Edit > Preferences > Behavior > Steps > Inset/Outset by.
  3. Isolate the Border:
    • Duplicate the original base shape again (Ctrl + D).
    • Select both the new duplicate and the inset shape.
    • Go to Path > Difference (Ctrl + -).
  4. Result: You now have an outline ring that hugs the inner boundary of your original shape. You can slice this ring using the Eraser Tool (in Cut mode) or the Pen Tool (Bezier) combined with Path > Division to keep shadows only where needed.

Method 3: Custom Shading with Clipping Masks

For complex cartoon illustrations where you want to freely draw shadow regions with the Pen tool without worrying about going outside the lines, clipping masks are the most flexible option.

  1. Duplicate the Base Shape: Select your base fill and press Ctrl + D. Move this duplicate to the side for a moment to serve as your clipping mask.
  2. Draw the Shadow: Use the Draw Freehand Lines Tool (Pencil) or Bezier Curves Tool (Pen) to draw a custom shadow shape. Extend the outer edges well beyond the boundaries of your character's body part; only the internal edge needs to be precise.
  3. Color the Shadow: Set the fill to your desired shadow color and remove any stroke outline.
  4. Align the Clip Mask: Move your duplicated base shape directly over the original illustration area.
  5. Apply the Clip: Select both your newly drawn shadow shape and the duplicated base shape. Go to Object > Clip > Set Clip.

The shadow is now automatically constrained within the exact contours of the base shape, leaving a clean inset appearance underneath your main outline strokes.
