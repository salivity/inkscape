Randomize Color Luminance of Objects in Inkscape

This guide explains how to quickly randomize the color luminance (lightness) of multiple selected vector objects in Inkscape without changing their base hues or saturations. By utilizing Inkscape’s built-in color manipulation extensions, you can introduce subtle or dramatic brightness variations across complex patterns, grids, and illustrations in just a few clicks.

Step 1: Select Your Objects

Using the Selector Tool (shortcut: S), click and drag around all the objects whose luminance you want to modify. Ensure the target objects are standard vector shapes or paths with a flat fill applied. If your shapes are grouped, ungroup them by pressing Ctrl + Shift + G (or Cmd + Shift + G on macOS), as some color extensions only process individual paths.

Step 2: Open the Randomize Extension

Navigate to the top menu bar and select Extensions > Color > Randomize.... A dialog box will appear displaying multiple adjustment sliders based on the HSL (Hue, Saturation, Lightness) color model.

Step 3: Configure the Lightness Settings

To alter only the luminance while keeping the original colors intact:

  1. Set the Hue range (%) to 0.
  2. Set the Saturation range (%) to 0.
  3. Adjust the Lightness range (%) slider to your preferred variance level. For subtle brightness shifts, enter a value between 10 and 25. For stark, high-contrast variations, set the value higher.

Step 4: Preview and Apply

Check the Live preview checkbox at the bottom of the dialog window to evaluate the luminance spread across your selected shapes in real time. If the distribution does not look right, you can toggle the preview or re-enter the lightness percentage to generate a new random seed. Once satisfied with the results, click Apply and close the dialog window.
