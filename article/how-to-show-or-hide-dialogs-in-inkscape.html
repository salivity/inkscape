How to Show or Hide Dialogs in Inkscape

Managing your workspace efficiently requires knowing how to display, minimize, and close dockable dialogs in Inkscape. This guide explains how to open specific tool panels such as Fill and Stroke or Layers, close or undock individual panels, collapse the entire docking panel, and use keyboard shortcuts to quickly toggle dialog visibility.

Opening Specific Dialogs

To open a specific dialog panel:

  1. Use the Menu Bar: Click on the relevant top-level menu. For example, select Object > Fill and Stroke, Layer > Layers and Objects, or Text > Text and Font.
  2. Use Keyboard Shortcuts: Most major dialogs have dedicated shortcuts:
    • Fill and Stroke: Ctrl + Shift + F (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + F (macOS)
    • Layers and Objects: Ctrl + Shift + L (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + L (macOS)
    • Align and Distribute: Ctrl + Shift + A (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + A (macOS)
    • Export: Ctrl + Shift + E (Windows/Linux) or Cmd + Shift + E (macOS)

Once opened, the dialog appears in the dock on the right side of the screen.

Closing an Individual Dialog

To remove a specific dialog from the dock:

  • Click the small X icon located directly on the tab of the active dialog.
  • Alternatively, enter the dialog's specific keyboard shortcut again or unselect it from the top menu to toggle it off.

Hiding and Showing All Dialogs Instantly

To maximize canvas space without losing your docked dialog arrangement:

  • Keyboard Shortcut: Press F12 to toggle the visibility of all open dialogs simultaneously. Pressing F12 again restores them in their previous layout.
  • Collapse Arrow: Click the small triangular arrow icon located on the divider between the canvas and the dialog dock to collapse or expand the dock.

Undocking and Floating Dialogs

  • Make a Dialog Float: Click and drag the dialog's tab away from the dock into the canvas area.
  • Redock a Dialog: Drag the floating window by its title bar back toward the right edge of the Inkscape window until a blue highlight indicates a valid dock area, then release the mouse.
