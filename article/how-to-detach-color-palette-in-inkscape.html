How to Detach Color Palette in Inkscape

This guide explains how to convert the Inkscape color palette into a standalone, floating window to customize your workspace. While the default color bar pinned to the bottom of the screen cannot be directly dragged away from the interface, you can achieve a detached, floating color palette by opening the Swatches dialog, undocking it from the side panel, and optionally hiding the default bottom bar to maximize your canvas area.

Step 1: Open the Swatches Dialog

The Swatches dialog contains the full color palette interface and can be manipulated as an independent window.

  1. Navigate to the top menu bar and click Object.
  2. Select Swatches from the dropdown menu (or press Ctrl + Shift + W on Windows/Linux, or Cmd + Shift + W on macOS).
  3. The Swatches panel will open, typically docked on the right side of your workspace.

Step 2: Undock the Palette into a Floating Window

Once the Swatches panel is visible, you can separate it from the main interface:

  1. Click and hold the Swatches tab title at the top of the docked panel.
  2. Drag your cursor away from the docking area toward the center of your screen.
  3. Release the mouse button. The palette is now a standalone floating window that you can resize and reposition anywhere, including on a secondary monitor.

Step 3: Customize the Floating Palette

With the palette floating, you can adjust its appearance to match your workflow:

  1. Click the small menu icon (represented by an arrow or three dots) located inside the floating Swatches window.
  2. Choose your preferred palette preset (such as Inkscape default, Web colors, or Custom).
  3. Adjust the tile size, border, and row count to make colors easier to select.

Step 4: Hide the Bottom Palette Bar (Optional)

To avoid having duplicate color bars and to reclaim vertical screen space:

  1. Go to View in the top menu.
  2. Hover over Show/Hide.
  3. Click Palette to uncheck it and remove the bottom bar from the interface.
