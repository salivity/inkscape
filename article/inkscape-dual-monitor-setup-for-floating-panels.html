Inkscape Dual Monitor Setup for Floating Panels

Configuring Inkscape for a dual-monitor workflow allows you to dedicate one entire screen to your active canvas while organizing tool palettes, layers, and formatting dialogs on the secondary display. By adjusting specific window preferences, undocking default panels, and saving your workspace geometry, you can prevent panels from snapping back to the canvas and ensure Inkscape remembers your multi-screen configuration across work sessions.

Step 1: Adjust Window Geometry Preferences

To stop Inkscape from automatically re-docking your panels upon relaunch, you must configure how the program manages window states.

  1. Open Inkscape and navigate to Edit > Preferences (or press Ctrl + Shift + P; on macOS, use Inkscape > Preferences or Cmd + ,).
  2. In the left-hand sidebar, select Interface > Windows.
  3. Under Window state, choose Remember and use last window's geometry to ensure both main and secondary windows maintain their size and screen positions.
  4. Under Dialog behavior, verify your preference:
    • Select Floating if you want newly opened dialogs to generate as independent windows automatically.
    • If you prefer grouping panels together on your second monitor, keep it on Dockable, which allows you to dock panels into a unified floating frame separate from the main canvas window.
  5. Close the Preferences window to apply the changes.

Step 2: Undock and Move Panels to the Second Monitor

Inkscape docks dialogs like Fill and Stroke, Align and Distribute, and Layers to the right edge of the main document window by default.

  1. Open the panels you regularly use (e.g., press Ctrl + Shift + F for Fill and Stroke or Ctrl + Shift + L for Layers and Objects).
  2. Click and hold the header tab of the open panel, then drag it away from the main canvas window toward your second monitor.
  3. Release the mouse cursor. The panel will now sit as a floating window on the second display.
  4. To combine multiple panels on your secondary display without cluttering the screen, drag subsequent panels directly into the header or side edges of the first floating panel until a blue drop target appears. This creates a dedicated multi-panel dock window on your second monitor.

Step 3: Expand the Document Window

Once the panels are moved to the secondary screen:

  1. Maximize or expand the primary Inkscape document window to fill the entire first display.
  2. The main canvas will now utilize your monitor's full aspect ratio without panel sidebars reducing horizontal workspace.
  3. You can press F12 at any time to toggle the visibility of all floating dialogs simultaneously without losing their monitor positioning.

Step 4: Save the Layout

To ensure your layout persists reliably:

  1. Close Inkscape using the standard File > Close or the window's close button while the panels remain open on your second monitor. Inkscape commits window coordinates on a clean exit.
  2. If you want specific dialogs to automatically open alongside new files in this layout, arrange your setup in an empty document, go to File > Save Template..., name your template, and check Set as default template.
