How to Make Laser Rubber Stamps in Inkscape

This guide explains how to prepare artwork in Inkscape for laser-etched rubber stamps featuring inverted mirror relief. Creating a functional stamp requires reversing your artwork so the stamped impression reads correctly, and inverting the raster colors so the laser vaporizes the surrounding rubber while leaving your text and icons raised. Following these direct steps ensures proper relief depth, clean borders, and crisp stamp impressions.

Step 1: Set Up the Document

  1. Open Inkscape and open Document Properties (Ctrl + Shift + D).
  2. Set the Display units and Page size units to millimeters or inches, depending on your laser cutter's preferences.
  3. Set the custom width and height to match the intended dimensions of your physical stamp block.

Step 2: Create and Finalize the Vector Artwork

  1. Place your text, graphics, or imported vectors within the canvas.
  2. Convert all text to paths by selecting the text and choosing Path > Object to Path (Ctrl + Shift + C).
  3. If your design consists of multiple overlapping shapes, select them and navigate to Path > Union (Ctrl + +) to merge them into a single solid path.

Step 3: Mirror the Design (Horizontal Flip)

Because a stamp transfers an impression face-down, your digital artwork must be reversed.

  1. Select your entire design by pressing Ctrl + A.
  2. Press the H key on your keyboard, or navigate to Object > Flip Horizontal.
  3. Verify that all text and directional graphics are now reversed.

Step 4: Create the Inverted Relief

Laser cutters interpret black as areas to engrave and white as areas to leave untouched. To make a relief stamp, the laser must vaporize the background and leave the design elements at the original surface height.

  1. Select the Rectangle tool (R) and draw a box that completely covers your artwork or matches the outer border of the stamp.
  2. Open the Fill and Stroke panel (Ctrl + Shift + Y).
  3. Set the rectangle's Fill to solid black (#000000) and set the Stroke to none.
  4. Send this rectangle behind your artwork by pressing the End key or navigating to Object > Lower to Bottom.
  5. Select your mirrored artwork paths sitting on top of the black rectangle.
  6. Change the Fill color of your artwork to pure white (#ffffff) and ensure the stroke is set to none.

The black background will now be engraved away by the laser, leaving the white areas raised to form the stamp face.

Step 5: Add a Vector Cut Line

To cut the rubber stamp out from the sheet during the same job:

  1. Draw a shape (rectangle, rounded rectangle, or circle) around the black background block.
  2. In the Fill and Stroke panel, set the Fill to none.
  3. Set the Stroke paint to solid red (#FF0000) or the color designated for vector cutting in your laser software.
  4. Set the Stroke style width to a hairline value (typically 0.1 mm or 0.001 in).

Step 6: Export the File

  1. Navigate to File > Save As.
  2. Save your file as an Inkscape SVG or Plain SVG. Most laser software packages, such as LightBurn or laser control plugins, accept standard SVG files directly.
  3. If your laser system requires a raster image for engraving, export the selection via File > Export PNG Image, ensuring the resolution is set to at least 500 to 600 DPI for sharp edges.
